# Statement


Let $F/\mathbb Q_p$ be finite. Let $\mathbf G$ be a split connected reductive group over $\overline{\mathbb Z}_p$ with connected center, maximal torus $\mathbf T$, simple roots $\Delta$, and Coxeter number $h_{\mathbf G}$, and assume $p>h_{\mathbf G}$. Let $\mathbf M\subsetneq\mathbf G$ be the standard Levi defined by $\Delta_M\subsetneq\Delta$, and let $U^{\mathbf M}$ be the unipotent radical of the corresponding standard parabolic. Choose a Tits representative $\dot w\in N_{\mathbf M}(\mathbf T)(\overline{\mathbb Z}_p)$, write $w$ for its Weyl image, assume $w$ is elliptic in $\mathbf M$, let $n$ be the order of $\dot w$, and let $F_n/F$ be the unramified extension of degree $n$. For each $\delta\in\Delta\setminus\Delta_M$, put $\boldsymbol\delta=\langle w\rangle\delta$, let $m_\delta=\#\boldsymbol\delta$, choose a root parametrization $x_\delta:\mathbb G_a\xrightarrow{\sim}U_\delta$, and define $c_\delta\in\overline{\mathbb Z}_p^\times$ by $\operatorname{Ad}(\dot w^{m_\delta})x_\delta(u)=x_\delta(c_\delta u)$. Choose cocharacters $\mu_\delta\in X_*(\mathbf T)$ satisfying $\langle\gamma,\mu_\delta\rangle=1$ for $\gamma=\delta$ and $0$ for every $\gamma\in\Delta\setminus\{\delta\}$, choose $z_\delta\in\overline{\mathbb Z}_p^\times$ with $z_\delta^{m_\delta}=c_\delta^{-1}$, and put $\mu=\sum_{\delta\notin\Delta_M}\mu_\delta$ and $t=\prod_{\delta\notin\Delta_M}\mu_\delta(z_\delta)$. Let $v:G_F\to\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ be the unramified quotient normalized by arithmetic Frobenius, let $q_n$ be its reduction modulo $n$, let $\kappa(g)=t^{v(g)}$, and let $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}:G_F\to\mathbb Z_p^\times$ be the cyclotomic character, equivalently the $\mathbb Q_p$-Lubin-Tate character. Then
\[
N(g)=\kappa(g)\,\mu(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g))\,\dot w^{q_n(g)}
\]
is a continuous homomorphism $G_F\to\mathbf T(\overline{\mathbb Z}_p)\langle\dot w\rangle$. For every $\delta\in\Delta\setminus\Delta_M$, if $F_{m_\delta}/F$ is the unramified extension of degree $m_\delta$, then the induced additive root-orbit module satisfies
\[
U_{\boldsymbol\delta}(\overline{\mathbb Z}_p)\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{G_{F_{m_\delta}}}^{G_F}\overline{\mathbb Z}_p(1),\qquad
H^2(G_F,U_{\boldsymbol\delta}(\overline{\mathbb Z}_p))\cong\overline{\mathbb Z}_p.
\]
On every positive root $\alpha=\sum_{\gamma\in\Delta}a_\gamma\gamma$ occurring in $U^{\mathbf M}$, the labeled Hodge-Tate weight is $-\langle\alpha,\mu\rangle=-\sum_{\delta\notin\Delta_M}\,a_\delta<0$. The resulting integral normalizer representation has cyclotomic stabilizer character on every omitted-simple-root orbit, so each orbit has free rank-one second Galois cohomology.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

### Integral cocharacters and the finite correction

Put $Q=\mathbb Z\Delta\subset X^*(\mathbf T)$. Connectedness of the center says that $X^*(\mathbf T)/Q$ is torsion-free. Dualizing therefore makes $X_*(\mathbf T)\to\operatorname{Hom}(Q,\mathbb Z)$ surjective, which supplies every $\mu_\delta$ in the statement. Since $\mu_\delta$ pairs trivially with $\Delta_M$, it is fixed by $W_M$. Hence $\mu$ and $t$ are fixed by $w$, and for every $\alpha\in\boldsymbol\delta$ one has $\langle\alpha,\mu\rangle=1$ and $\alpha(t)=z_\delta$.

The orbit size $m_\delta$ divides $n$. Since $\dot w^n=1$, the scalar $c_\delta$ satisfies $c_\delta^{n/m_\delta}=1$. Any choice $z_\delta^{m_\delta}=c_\delta^{-1}$ consequently satisfies $z_\delta^n=1$. Thus $t^n=1$, so $g\mapsto t^{v(g)}$ is a well-defined finite unramified integral character. The cited Weyl-order lemma and the fact that a Tits representative has order equal to, or twice, its Weyl order show that $p\nmid n$ under $p>h_{\mathbf G}$; all the finite correction factors are therefore prime-to-$p$ units in a finite coefficient extension.

### Normalizer descent and Hodge weights

Both $\kappa(g)$ and $\mu(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g))$ lie in the $w$-fixed subtorus. They commute with $\dot w$, while $q_n$ is a homomorphism and $\dot w^n=1$. The displayed formula for $N$ is therefore a homomorphism, with no choice of coset expression and no missing relation at the $n$th power. Every factor is integral and continuous. On $G_{F_n}$ the two finite factors are trivial, so $N|_{G_{F_n}}=\mu\circ\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}$; in particular the representation is crystalline after the unramified extension $F_n/F$.

The cyclotomic character has labeled Hodge-Tate weight $-1$ at every embedding, whereas both finite factors have weight zero. If $\alpha$ is a positive root in $U^{\mathbf M}$, at least one coefficient outside $\Delta_M$ is positive. The weight calculation in the statement follows and verifies strict parabolic negativity, not merely integrality. This also explains why a single nonparallel Lubin-Tate character with arbitrary exponents is insufficient.

### The orbit module and local duality

The stabilizer of $U_\delta$ in $G_F$ is $G_{F_{m_\delta}}$. If $g$ is in this stabilizer and $q_n(g)=m_\delta\ell$, then $\dot w^{q_n(g)}$ acts on $U_\delta$ by $c_\delta^\ell$, while $\kappa(g)$ acts by
\[
\delta(t)^{m_\delta\ell}=z_\delta^{m_\delta\ell}=c_\delta^{-\ell}.
\]
These factors cancel. The remaining scalar is $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g)$ because $\langle\delta,\mu\rangle=1$. Thus the stabilizer root line is exactly $\overline{\mathbb Z}_p(1)$, including its unramified action, and transitivity on the root factors gives the asserted induced-module isomorphism.

Shapiro's lemma now gives
\[
H^2(G_F,U_{\boldsymbol\delta})\cong H^2(G_{F_{m_\delta}},\overline{\mathbb Z}_p(1)).
\]
For every sufficiently large finite coefficient ring $\mathcal O\subset\overline{\mathbb Z}_p$, the local invariant map, equivalently integral local Tate duality, identifies $H^2(G_{F_{m_\delta}},\mathcal O(1))$ with $\mathcal O$. Scalar extension through the finite coefficient rings gives $\overline{\mathbb Z}_p$. This proves freeness of rank one, rather than only nonvanishing after inverting $p$.

## Post-proof remarks

Verified corrected construction. It replaces the arbitrary nonparallel $F_n$-Lubin-Tate cocharacter by the parallel cyclotomic character (the $\mathbb Q_p$ Lubin-Tate character), uses connected center to make the relative cocharacter integral, and includes the finite unramified torus correction needed to cancel Tits-representative monodromy on every omitted-simple-root orbit. The construction covers arbitrary finite $F/\mathbb Q_p$, every standard proper Levi, and every elliptic Tits representative under the standing connected-center and $p>h_{\mathbf G}$ hypotheses. It does not assert that the old arbitrary cocharacter works, and it does not address the separate formal representability, projective-dimension, descent, or special-fiber claims downstream.

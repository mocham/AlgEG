# Statement


Let $F/\mathbb Q_p$ be finite, let $\mathbf G$ be a reductive group in the integral Emerton-Gee setting, and fix a bounded Hodge type and inertial type. The morphism from the stack of crystalline Breuil-Kisin-Fargues lattices of that type to the integral Emerton-Gee stack is proper. Its scheme-theoretic image is $\mathbb Z_p$-flat, and a geometric point of its special fiber belongs to that image if and only if the corresponding residual representation has a crystalline lift of the fixed type.

## Expanded self-contained proof

### Expanded proof

Let $\mathcal C^{\lambda,\tau}_{\mathrm{ss}}$ be the flat fixed-type Breuil--Kisin--Fargues lattice stack used in F09-004. Imposing $N=0$ gives the closed crystalline condition, and the fixed-type crystalline lattice stack is its flat part $\mathcal C^{\lambda,\tau}_{\mathrm{crys}}$. The crystalline construction is compatible with base change and is a closed substack of the semistable construction. Since the semistable lattice morphism to the integral Emerton--Gee stack is representable, proper, and of finite presentation, its restriction to $\mathcal C^{\lambda,\tau}_{\mathrm{crys}}$ has the same properties. F09-001 supplies the required algebraicity of the target.

Let $\mathcal Z$ be the scheme-theoretic image. Locally on an affine or versal presentation of the target, $\mathcal O_{\mathcal Z}$ is the image of $\mathcal O_{\mathcal X}$ in the proper pushforward of $\mathcal O_{\mathcal C_{\mathrm{crys}}}$. Multiplication by $p$ is injective on the latter because the crystalline lattice stack is flat, hence is injective on this image. Thus $\mathcal O_{\mathcal Z}$ is $p$-torsion-free and therefore flat over the coefficient discrete valuation ring, in particular over $\mathbb Z_p$. This is the crystalline $N=0$ specialization of the flat-image argument in F09-004.

The versal comparison used in F09-004 is unchanged after imposing $N=0$: the completed image at a residual point is the unique flat fixed-Hodge-type, fixed-inertial-type crystalline quotient of the framed deformation ring. Its finite-flat points are exactly integral representations whose generic fibers have $N=0$, namely potentially crystalline representations of the fixed type. Hence a crystalline lift, after choosing a stable lattice and enlarging coefficients if necessary, gives a finite-flat point of the lattice stack and places its reduction in $\mathcal Z_{\mathbb F_p}$. Conversely, if a geometric residual point lies in $\mathcal Z_{\mathbb F_p}$, flatness of its versal crystalline quotient gives a nonempty generic fiber; a classical point of that generic fiber is a crystalline lift of the prescribed type. This proves the pointwise characterization.

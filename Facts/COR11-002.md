# Statement


Let $k$ be a field and let
\[
R=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n,u_1^{\pm1},\ldots,u_s^{\pm1}]
\]
be graded by polynomial degree with $\deg x_i=1$ and $\deg u_j^{\pm1}=0$. Let $L\subset R$ be generated by finitely many homogeneous elements, each of which has degree zero or one. For $1\le i\le m$, let $q_i$ be homogeneous of degree two and let $h_i$ have only monomials of degree at least three. Put
\[
I=L+(q_1+h_1,\ldots,q_m+h_m),\qquad J=L+(q_1,\ldots,q_m).
\]
Then
\[
\dim R/J\geq\dim R/I.
\]
Consequently, for a fine model whose fixed linear equations are retained unchanged and whose obstruction equations are replaced only by their complete quadratic parts, the corrected cone model has dimension at least the dimension of the fine model.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

Give every $x_i$ weight $-1$ and every Laurent variable $u_j$ weight zero, and use the convention that $\operatorname{in}_w(f)$ is the sum of terms of maximal weight. Every generator of $L$ is weight-homogeneous. If $q_i\ne0$, its terms have weight $-2$, while every term of $h_i$ has weight at most $-3$, so
\[
\operatorname{in}_w(q_i+h_i)=q_i.
\]
If $q_i=0$, that index contributes no generator to $J$ and no initial-form assertion is needed. Thus every generator of $J$ lies in the weighted initial ideal and
\[
J\subseteq\operatorname{in}_w(I).
\]
This inclusion, rather than an unsupported equality of initial ideals, is all that is needed.

For completeness, form the weighted homogenization with a parameter $z$: for each polynomial, multiply its monomials by the nonnegative powers of $z$ that raise them to its maximal $w$-weight, and take the saturated homogenized ideal. This is the usual Rees/Groebner construction for an integer weight vector. Its localization at $z$ is $(R/I)[z,z^{-1}]$, while its fiber at $z=0$ is $R/\operatorname{in}_w(I)$. The Rees algebra is flat over $k[z]$; Laurent localization in the weight-zero $u_j$ commutes with the construction. Therefore
\[
\dim R/\operatorname{in}_w(I)=\dim R/I.
\]
The ideal inclusion gives a surjection
\[
R/J\twoheadrightarrow R/\operatorname{in}_w(I),
\]
so Krull dimension cannot increase after taking that quotient. Hence
\[
\dim R/J\geq\dim R/\operatorname{in}_w(I)=\dim R/I.
\]

For the corrected fine/cone pair, first quotient by their common linear ideal. Since $t_\alpha-1$ is common, the term $s_\alpha(t_\alpha-1)$ may be removed from each obstruction generator on both sides. The remaining fine generator is $q_\alpha+h_\alpha$ and its cone generator is $q_\alpha$, so the theorem applies verbatim. No matching, specialization of simple-root coordinates, equality of initial ideals, or Groebner basis for the full fine ideal is required.

## Faithful GrobV14 proof

```tex
\begin{proof}
\EDITXIV{In the common coordinate ring $A$, let $L$ be the common linear
ideal generated by the equations of degree $0$ and $1$. The fine and cone ideals are
\[
I=L+(q_\alpha+h_\alpha)_\alpha,
\qquad
J=L+(q_\alpha)_\alpha.
\]
Give the polynomial variables weight $-1$ and the Laurent variables
weight $0$. Then
\[
J\subseteq\operatorname{in}_w(I).
\]
Weighted homogenization gives a flat degeneration from $A/I$ to
$A/\operatorname{in}_w(I)$. Therefore
\[
\dim A/I=\dim A/\operatorname{in}_w(I)\le\dim A/J,
\]
which is the required inequality.}
\end{proof}
```

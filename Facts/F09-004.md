# Statement

Let G be a reductive group in the setting of integral L-parameters, and fix a Hodge type lambda and inertial type tau. The fixed Hodge and inertial type potentially semistable stack is a finite-type flat p-adic formal algebraic stack, characterized by its finite flat points, and its mod p fiber is equidimensional of dimension dim(G/P_lambda).

# Proof or Examples

## Expanded self-contained proof

### Expanded proof

Choose a height bound h for lambda and a finite Galois extension L/E on whose inertia tau is trivial. Let C^{lambda,tau} be the corresponding flat fixed Hodge and inertial type Breuil--Kisin--Fargues stack. Proposition B.5.10 in the cited manuscript range says that C^{lambda,tau} is a p-adic formal algebraic stack of finite presentation, flat over Spf O, and that its morphism to the integral L-parameter stack X is representable by algebraic spaces, proper, and of finite presentation. Define X^{lambda,tau} to be its scheme-theoretic image, as in B.5.11. General algebraicity of X and the proper finite-presentation image construction show that X^{lambda,tau} is a finite-type p-adic formal algebraic stack. Its structure sheaf is, by the definition of scheme-theoretic image, the image of O_X in the pushforward of O_{C^{lambda,tau}}. This image has no O-torsion because C^{lambda,tau} is O-flat; over the discrete valuation ring O, torsion-free modules are flat. Thus X^{lambda,tau} is O-flat.

For a finite flat O-algebra A, Proposition B.5.10 identifies the A-points of C^{lambda,tau} with Breuil--Kisin--Fargues modules of Hodge type lambda and inertial type tau. Lemma B.6.2 compares the formal completion of the image at every residual L-parameter with the framed fixed Hodge and inertial type potentially semistable deformation ring. Together with the uniqueness characterization of that O-flat quotient in Theorem B.6.1, this gives exactly: an A-point of X factors through X^{lambda,tau} if and only if its generic-fiber L-parameter is potentially semistable of Hodge type lambda and inertial type tau. The same local comparison, or equivalently the cited uniqueness result for flat closed substacks, shows that these finite-flat points uniquely determine X^{lambda,tau}.

Finally fix a residual point rho-bar. By Lemma B.6.2 there is a framed versal morphism Spf R_{rho-bar}^{square,tau,lambda} -> X^{lambda,tau}. Theorem B.6.1 says that this O-flat ring is equidimensional of dimension 1 + dim(G) + dim(G/P_lambda). Passing from the framed versal space to the stack subtracts dim(G), the dimension of the framing group. Hence X^{lambda,tau} is locally equidimensional of dimension 1 + dim(G/P_lambda). Since p is a nonzerodivisor on this flat stack, passage to the mod p fiber subtracts one more dimension. Every residual point has such a versal neighborhood, so the whole special fiber is equidimensional of dimension dim(G/P_lambda).

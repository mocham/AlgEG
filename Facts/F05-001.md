# Statement


Let $p$ be a prime, let $R=\mathbb{Z}_p\langle T\rangle$, and put $X=\operatorname{Spf}R$. On the big affine formal site over $X$, define the presheaf $\mathcal F$ on $p$-adically complete $R$-algebras by $\mathcal F(S)=\operatorname{Tor}^R_1(R/(T),S)$. Then $\mathcal F$ is represented by the formal scheme $\operatorname{Spf}(R\langle Y\rangle/(TY))$.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

Because $T$ is a nonzerodivisor in $R=\mathbb Z_p\langle T\rangle$, the sequence $0\to R\xrightarrow{T}R\to R/(T)\to0$ is a free resolution of $R/(T)$. After tensoring over $R$ with a $p$-adically complete $R$-algebra $S$, its degree-one homology is $\operatorname{Tor}^R_1(R/(T),S)=\ker(T:S\to S)=\{s\in S:Ts=0\}$. This identification is natural in $S$. Put $A=R\langle Y\rangle/(TY)$, with its $p$-adic topology. The universal property of the restricted power-series algebra gives $\operatorname{Hom}^{\mathrm{cont}}_{R\text{-alg}}(A,S)\cong\{y\in S:Ty=0\}$ by sending a homomorphism to the image of $Y$. Conversely, any such $y$ defines a continuous evaluation homomorphism: a restricted series $\sum a_iY^i$ converges after evaluation because $a_i\to0$ $p$-adically and multiplication by powers of $y$ preserves every ideal $p^N S$. The relation $TY=0$ is respected exactly when $Ty=0$. These bijections are functorial in $S$, so Yoneda identifies the presheaf with the functor of points of $\operatorname{Spf}(A)$.

## Faithful GrobV14 proof

```tex
\begin{proof}
First, compute the functor
$F(S) = \mathrm{Tor}^R_1(R/(T), S)$.
Since $T$ is a non-zero-divisor in $R$,
we have a free resolution:
\[ 0 \to R \xrightarrow{\cdot T} R \to
R/(T) \to 0 \]
Tensoring with an $R$-algebra $S$,
we get the complex:
\[ 0 \to S \xrightarrow{\cdot T} S \to
S/TS \to 0 \]
The first homology group is
$\ker(S \xrightarrow{\cdot T} S) = S[T]$.
Thus, $F(S)$ assigns each $S$ its
$T$-torsion submodule, $S[T]$.

To represent this functor, consider
the $p$-adically complete $R$-algebra
$A = R\langle Y \rangle / (TY)$.
A continuous $R$-algebra homomorphism
$\phi: A \to S$ is determined by
$y = \phi(Y) \in S$.
This requires $Ty = 0$ in $S$.
Because $A$ is a Tate algebra,
any such $y$ gives a continuous map.
This yields a bijection
$\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(A, S) \cong S[T]$.
\end{proof}
```

# Statement


Let $F/\Q_p$ be a finite extension, let $\bG$ be a connected reductive group, and let $\bB$ be a Borel subgroup of $\bG$. The reduced special fiber of a $\bG$-valued crystalline stack of regular Hodge type is equidimensional of dimension
\[
[F:\Q_p]\dim(\bG/\bB).
\]

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

### Proof reduction

Let $X_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{crys}}$ be the integral crystalline stack of the fixed regular Hodge type. By the crystalline Breuil--Kisin--Fargues image theorem F05-023, this is the flat scheme-theoretic image of the corresponding lattice stack. Fix a geometric residual point $x$ of its special fiber. A framed formal neighborhood of $x$ is governed by the monodromy-zero fixed-type deformation ring $R_x^{\square,\lambda,N=0}$.

By COR7-003, the generic fiber of this ring is regular and equidimensional of dimension

$$\dim(G)+\dim((\operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb Q_p}G)/P_{\lambda}).$$

Because $\lambda$ is regular, its stabilizer at every embedding $F\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb Q}_p$ is a Borel. Consequently

$$\dim((\operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb Q_p}G)/P_{\lambda})=[F:\mathbb Q_p]\dim(G/B).$$

The integral crystalline ring is flat over the coefficient discrete valuation ring. Thus every irreducible component is horizontal, its total dimension is one more than its generic-fiber dimension, and the uniformizer is a nonzerodivisor. Every irreducible component of the reduced special fiber therefore has dimension

$$\dim(G)+[F:\mathbb Q_p]\dim(G/B).$$

The framed formal neighborhood is a smooth atlas for the unframed stack with relative dimension $\dim(G)$. Subtracting that framing dimension shows that every component through $x$ has stack dimension $[F:\mathbb Q_p]\dim(G/B)$. Since every special-fiber point has such a framed formal neighborhood, the reduced special fiber is equidimensional of the asserted dimension.

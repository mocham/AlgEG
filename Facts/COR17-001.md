# Statement


Let $K/\mathbb Q_p$ be finite, let $\mathcal O$ be the integers in a finite extension of $\mathbb Q_p$ with uniformizer $\varpi$, put $A=\mathcal O\langle T^{\pm1}\rangle$, and let $\operatorname{ur}_T:G_K\to A^\times$ be the unramified character sending arithmetic Frobenius to $T$. Let $M_T$ be the etale $(\varphi,\Gamma)$-module attached to $A(1)\otimes_A\operatorname{ur}_T$. Then there is a natural isomorphism

$$H^2_{\mathrm{Herr}}(M_T)\cong A/(T-1),$$

compatible with reduction modulo every $\varpi^a$, with finite-type coefficient base change at each such level, and with passage to the rigid generic fiber. More generally, if $B$ is an affine coefficient algebra, $u\in B^\times$, and $L$ is an invertible $B$-module such that a rank-one family is obtained from $M_T$ by $T\mapsto u$ and tensoring with $L$, then, whenever the indicated integral family and its levelwise base changes are defined,

$$H^2_{\mathrm{Herr}}(M_T\widehat\otimes_{A,T\mapsto u}L)\cong L/(u-1)L.$$

The isomorphism is functorial for coefficient base change and for automorphisms of the character family. Thus $u-1$ is the equation pulled back from the trivial-character Cartier divisor, with exponent one. If $u-1$ and its reduction modulo $\varpi$ are nonzerodivisors, this is a simple Cartier-torsion module with a length-one line-bundle resolution before and after reduction.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

For $A_a=A/\varpi^a$, EG23 proves that the Herr complex is perfect in degrees $[0,2]$, that its derived formation and its top cohomology commute with the relevant arbitrary finite-type base changes, and that $H^2(A_a(1))=A_a$. Its family Tate-duality quasi-isomorphism identifies the top end of the complex for $A_a(1)\otimes\operatorname{ur}_T$ with the dual of the degree-zero end for its Cartier dual $\operatorname{ur}_T^{-1}$.

The degree-zero condition is scheme-theoretic, not just pointwise. After any coefficient base change $A_a\to C$, an invariant section of the Cartier dual is an element $c\in C$ satisfying $(T^{-1}-1)c=0$, equivalently $(T-1)c=0$. Hence the invariant block is multiplication by the single equation $T-1$ cutting out the identity in the unramified-character torus. Dualizing that block makes the top cohomology its cokernel, $A_a/(T-1)$. The construction is natural, so tensoring by a line and pulling back $T$ to $u$ gives $L/(u-1)L$ and preserves all descent automorphisms. In particular, applying the calculation to infinitesimal coefficient algebras sees the actual equation $u-1$; replacing it by $(u-1)^e$ with $e>1$ would give the wrong Cartier-dual invariant functor on the first nontrivial infinitesimal thickening.

KPX Theorems 4.4.3 and 4.4.5 give the corresponding derived base change, perfectness, and family Tate duality over affinoid algebras. Their rank-one character calculation identifies degree-zero cohomology with the trivial character on the fixed-weight unramified slice. In the proof of the rank-one graph theorem, KPX also checks the scheme structure: the cohomology sheaves are locally monogenic and a hypothetical length-two Artinian thickening of the identity graph contradicts uniqueness of the Artinian character. Thus the rigid trivial-character graph is reduced and the generic-fiber quotient has exponent one, rather than merely being supported at $T=1$.

The compatible formulas over all $A_a$ pass to the $p$-adically complete perfect complex, giving $A/(T-1)$ integrally; the KPX calculation agrees after inverting $p$. Since $T-1$ is a nonzerodivisor in $A$ and remains one modulo $\varpi$, the displayed two-term line resolution is exact at both levels.

# Statement


Let \(F/\mathbb Q_p\) be finite and let \(\mathbf G\) be the split pinned connected reductive group with connected center in the manuscript's standing setup. Put
\[
X=\mathcal X_{\mathbf G,\mathrm{red}}^{\mathrm{EG}}.
\]
For each standard Levi \(\mathbf M\) and each elliptic Weyl class \(w\in W_{\mathbf M}\), choose its fixed Tits representative \(\dot w\) and let
\[
f_{\mathbf M,w}:\mathcal X_{U^{\mathbf M}\rtimes\mathbf T\langle\dot w\rangle,\mathrm{red}}^{\mathrm{EG}}
\longrightarrow X
\]
forget the twisted-Borel reduction. Form the single morphism from the finite disjoint union
\[
f:\coprod_{(\mathbf M,w)}
\mathcal X_{U^{\mathbf M}\rtimes\mathbf T\langle\dot w\rangle,\mathrm{red}}^{\mathrm{EG}}
\longrightarrow X.
\]
Then the reduced scheme-theoretic image of \(f\) is all of \(X\). Consequently every irreducible component of \(X\) contains a dense open dominated by an irreducible component of one of the displayed twisted-Borel reduction stacks.

Put \(D=[F:\mathbb Q_p]\#\Phi^+\). Assume either
\[
[F:\mathbb Q_p]\ge\#\Phi^++2,
\]
or that \(\mathbf G\) has type \(F_4\) in the ranges treated by the corrected Borel calculations. Then every irreducible component of \(X\), not merely every component already known to be top-dimensional, is the closure of the scheme-theoretic image of a full maximally nonsplit Borel locus. These components are uniquely indexed by
\[
\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal W_{\mathbf B}).
\]
In particular, \(X\) is equidimensional of dimension \(D\), the full maximally nonsplit images are pairwise distinct and exhaustive, and in type \(F_4\) over \(\mathbb Q_p\) every Serre-indexed exceptional row image is contained in the closure of one of these usual components.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

The index set is finite because there are finitely many standard Levis and finite Weyl groups. Let \(x\in X(\overline{\mathbb F}_p)\) correspond to \(\bar\rho\). By F09-033, choosing a parabolic \(P=U^{\mathbf M}\rtimes\mathbf M\) minimal among those containing the image makes the Levi projection \(\bar\rho_{\mathbf M}\) \(\mathbf M\)-irreducible, hence \(\mathbf M\)-completely reducible. Lin22 Theorem 4 makes that projection quasi-semisimple, and COR6-004 places it, after conjugacy, in \(\mathbf T\langle\dot w\rangle\) for an elliptic \(w\in W_{\mathbf M}\). Therefore
\[
\bar\rho(G_F)\subset U^{\mathbf M}\rtimes\mathbf T\langle\dot w\rangle.
\]
This is the definitional classification of the non-semisimple representation as a unipotent extension of an irreducible Levi representation.

COR17-007 gives finite type. Take the scheme-theoretic image of the single morphism from the finite disjoint union in the Statement. Its reduction is a closed reduced finite-type substack with every geometric point of the reduced target. A nonempty complement would have a geometric point on a finite-type smooth atlas, so the image equals \(X\). For any irreducible component \(Y\subset X\), finiteness of the disjoint union forces one source component to have dense image in \(Y\).

On component-generic full maximally nonsplit Borel loci, COR5-001 gives singleton fibers and the expected image dimension. Distinctness follows by restricting over disjoint dense opens of distinct Weil--Deligne components before intersecting their dense images. The corrected large-degree and \(F_4\) dimension estimates identify the component-dominating full loci in the stated ranges. COR18-001 lists every \(F_4/\mathbb Q_p\) exceptional Borel component, and COR8-002 rotates each Serre-indexed realization densely into a usual maximally nonsplit locus without changing its reductive image. Hence the usual images exhaust the reductive components and give equidimensionality in those ranges.

## Faithful GrobV14 proof

```tex
\begin{proof}
\label{fact:COR19-002}
\EDITXIV{Put $X=\cX_{\bG,\red}^{\EG}$.  There are finitely many standard
Levi subgroups and elliptic classes in their Weyl groups.  Given
$x\in X(\bFp)$, choose a parabolic $U^{\bM}\rtimes\bM$ minimal among
those containing the corresponding Galois image.  The Levi projection is
$\bM$-irreducible, and Theorem~\ref{fact:COR6-004} conjugates it into
$\bT\langle\wt w\rangle$ for an elliptic $w\in W_{\bM}$.  Hence the
reduced scheme-theoretic image of the morphism from the finite disjoint union
\[
\coprod_{(\bM,w)}
\cX_{U^{\bM}\rtimes\bT\langle\wt w\rangle,\red}^{\EG}
\longrightarrow X
\]
is $X$, so one irreducible component of one of these stacks maps densely
to each irreducible component of $X$.  Put $D=[F:\Q_p]\#\Phi^+$.
Theorems~\ref{fact:COR15-004},
\ref{fact:COR18-001}, and~\ref{fact:COR18-003}, together with
Proposition~\ref{fact:COR8-002} in type $\mathrm F_4$, show that only the
full maximally nonsplit Borel loci can map densely to components in the
two cases of the proposition, and that their dimension is $D$.

For each $C\in\operatorname{Irr}(\cW_{\bB})$, define the maximally
nonsplit locus over $C$ by the fiber product
\[
\dot\cX_{\bB}(C):=
\dot\cX_{\bB}\langle\Psi_0(C),\Psi_2(C)\rangle
\times_{\cW_{\bB}}C.
\]
Corollary~\ref{fact:COR15-003} makes this locus irreducible and dominant
over $C$, while Lemma~\ref{fact:COR5-001} gives image dimension $D$.
The finite argument above gives all irreducible components, and applying
the same lemma over disjoint dense opens of distinct $C$ shows that their
closures are distinct.}
\end{proof}
```

## Post-proof remarks

Verified using Lin22's definitions as imported in F09-033. The covering source is one finite disjoint union, and one takes its scheme-theoretic image. A minimal parabolic makes the Levi projection irreducible by definition; Lin22 is then applied only to that completely reducible Levi projection. This removes the former treatment of non-semisimple classification as an additional missing theorem.

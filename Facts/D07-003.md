# Statement


Let $\Gamma$ be a profinite group and let
\[
1\longrightarrow Z\longrightarrow\widetilde G\xrightarrow{\pi}G\longrightarrow1
\]
be a central extension of discrete topological groups with $Z$ abelian. Let $\rho:\Gamma\to G$ be continuous, assume that $\rho(\Gamma)$ is finite, and assume that every element of $\rho(\Gamma)$ has a lift to $\widetilde G$. There is a canonical obstruction class $o(\rho)\in H^2(\Gamma,Z)$. The representation $\rho$ admits a continuous lift $\widetilde\rho:\Gamma\to\widetilde G$ if and only if $o(\rho)=0$. When a lift exists, the set of lifts is a torsor under $H^1(\Gamma,Z)$. Consequently, constructing representations into separate factors of a central-isogeny cover does not by itself produce a representation into the covered group: one must also prove vanishing of the central obstruction and compatibility on the finite overlap.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

Choose lifts $g_\gamma\in\widetilde G$ of $\rho(\gamma)$, constant on the fibers of the finite quotient through which $\rho$ factors. The defect
\[
 z(\gamma,\eta)=g_\gamma g_\eta g_{\gamma\eta}^{-1}
\]
lies in $Z$ and is continuous. Associativity and centrality give the two-cocycle identity. Replacing $g_\gamma$ by $a_\gamma g_\gamma$ for a continuous one-cochain $a:\Gamma\to Z$ changes $z$ by the coboundary of $a$, so the class $o(\rho)=[z]$ is canonical.

The chosen lifts form a homomorphism exactly when $z=1$. They can therefore be corrected to a homomorphism exactly when $[z]=0$. If $\widetilde\rho_1$ and $\widetilde\rho_2$ are two lifts, the pointwise ratio $\widetilde\rho_2\widetilde\rho_1^{-1}$ is a continuous one-cocycle in $Z$; conversely every such cocycle twists one lift to another. This gives the stated $H^1$-torsor. The final sentence applies the criterion to the finite central overlap in a product cover.

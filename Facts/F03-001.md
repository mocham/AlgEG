# Statement


Let $\bB=U\rtimes\bT$ be a split Borel subgroup over $\bFp$, where $U$ is its unipotent radical and $\bT$ its maximal torus. Let $\Gamma\subset\bB(\bFp)$ be a subgroup, and let $K_1,K_2\subset U$ be $\bT$-stable normal subgroups. If, for each $i=1,2$, there is $b_i\in\bB(\bFp)$ such that $b_i\Gamma b_i^{-1}\subset (K_i\rtimes\bT)(\bFp)$, then there is $b\in\bB(\bFp)$ such that $b\Gamma b^{-1}\subset ((K_1\cap K_2)\rtimes\bT)(\bFp)$.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

### Proof

Write B_0=B(F_p), P=U(F_p), and A=T(F_p). Since B is split, P is a finite p-group, A is an abelian group of order prime to p, and B_0=P semidirect A. Put L_i=K_i(F_p). Normality in U and T-stability make each L_i normal in B_0.

Let N=Gamma intersect P. Since P is normal in B_0,

    b_i N b_i^{-1}=(b_i Gamma b_i^{-1}) intersect P subset L_i.

As L_i is normal in B_0, conjugating back shows N subset L_i for i=1,2. Thus N subset L:=L_1 intersect L_2. Moreover, N is a normal Hall p-subgroup of Gamma: the quotient Gamma/N embeds under the projection B_0 -> A and therefore has order prime to p.

Apply the Schur-Zassenhaus theorem. There is a complement H to N in Gamma, so Gamma=N semidirect H and H is a p-prime group. The projection pi:B_0 -> A is injective on H; let A_0=pi(H). Inside P semidirect A_0, both H and the standard copy A_0 are complements to the normal Hall subgroup P. The conjugacy clause of Schur-Zassenhaus gives u in P such that uHu^{-1}=A_0. Since L is normal in B_0, uNu^{-1} subset L. Consequently

    u Gamma u^{-1}=(uNu^{-1}) semidirect (uHu^{-1}) subset L semidirect A,

which is ((K_1 intersect K_2) semidirect T)(F_p). Taking b=u proves the claim.

\EDITXIV{Choose a finite field $k\subset\bFp$ over which the groups and
conjugating elements are defined.  Put $B_0=P\rtimes A$, where $P=U(k)$
is a finite $p$-group and $A=\bT(k)$ has order prime to $p$, and put
$L_i=K_i(k)$.  If $N=\Gamma\cap P$, normality gives
$N\subset L_1\cap L_2$, while $\Gamma/N$ has order prime to $p$.
By Schur--Zassenhaus, write $\Gamma=N\rtimes H$.  Inside
$P\rtimes\pi(H)$, the subgroups $H$ and $\pi(H)$ are complements to
$P$, so some $u\in P$ satisfies $uHu^{-1}=\pi(H)$.  Since
$L_1\cap L_2$ is normal, $uNu^{-1}\subset L_1\cap L_2$, and hence
$u\Gamma u^{-1}\subset(L_1\cap L_2)\rtimes A$.}
\end{proof}

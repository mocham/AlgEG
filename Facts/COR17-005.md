# Statement


Let $F/\mathbb Q_p$ be finite, put $d_F=[F:\mathbb Q_p]$, and let $\mathbf G$ be a split connected reductive group over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$ with connected center and root system $\Phi$. Assume $p>h_{\mathbf G}$, fix a Borel subgroup $\mathbf B=U\rtimes\mathbf T$ with positive roots $\Phi^+$, and let $K\subset U$ be a $\mathbf T$-stable normal subgroup with root set $\Phi_K$. For a fine configuration $\Psi=(\Psi_0,\Psi_2)$, let $\mathcal X_{\mathbf B}[K]\langle\Psi\rangle$ be the corresponding locally closed fine Borel stratum. Set

\[
N=\#\Phi^+,\qquad \ell=\operatorname{rank}\Phi,\qquad m=\#\Phi_K,\qquad S=\Phi_K\cap\Psi_2,\qquad a=\#S,
\]

\[
q=\dim_{\mathbb Q}\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb Q}(\Psi_2),\qquad s=\#((\Phi^+\setminus\Phi_K)\cap\Psi_0).
\]

Then the complete fine-equation presentation and the change-of-group stabilizer formula give

\[
\dim \mathcal X_{\mathbf B}[K]\langle\Psi\rangle\le d_Fm+\ell+a-q-s.
\]

The vector matroid on $\Phi^+$ satisfies $a-q\le N-\ell$, and therefore

\[
d_FN-\dim \mathcal X_{\mathbf B}[K]\langle\Psi\rangle\ge d_F(N-m)-N+s.
\]

Thus, relative to the top Borel dimension $d_FN$, every stratum with $K\ne U$ has strict positive codimension when $d_F\ge N+1$, and in particular when $d_F\ge N+2$. Every non-full fine Borel stratum has positive codimension already when $d_F\ge N+1$.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

Put $d_k=[k_{F_{\mathrm{cyc}}}:\mathbb F_p]$. COR10-002 gives the zero-dimensional general Borel Weil-Deligne base and the rank-one destackified coordinate count: a recursive root block has $d_F+d_k$ additive coordinates before the $d_k$-dimensional gauge factor is removed. In the corrected presentation of COR14-002 there are consequently $(d_F+d_k)m$ root coordinates $x_{\alpha,i}$, $\ell$ torus coordinates $t_\delta$ indexed by the simple roots, and one coordinate $s_\alpha$ for each $\alpha\in\Phi_K$.

The linear equations $s_\alpha=0$ for $\alpha\notin\Psi_2$ leave only the $a=\#(\Phi_K\cap\Psi_2)$ coordinates indexed by $S$. The character equations $t_\alpha-1=0$ for $\alpha\in\Psi_2$ cut the torus-coordinate space in codimension $q=\dim_{\mathbb Q}\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb Q}(\Psi_2)$. Ignore the possibly additional character cuts from $\Psi_0$, and also ignore every terminal equation

\[
r_\alpha=s_\alpha(t_\alpha-1)+A_\alpha
\]

for $\alpha\in\Psi_2$ of $K$-height greater than one. Removing equations can only enlarge dimension, so COR14-002 gives the fine-chart bound

\[
\dim \dot X_{K\rtimes\mathbf T}\langle\Psi\rangle\le (d_F+d_k)m+\ell+a-q.
\]

The full class-by-class BCH terms occur only in the omitted terminal equations for this count. Keeping those equations, with all their BCH terms, can only lower this upper bound.

COR15-001 gives the exact change-of-group formula

\[
\dim\mathcal X_{\mathbf B}[K]\langle\Psi\rangle=\dim\dot X_{K\rtimes\mathbf T}\langle\Psi\rangle-d_km-s.
\]

Substitution cancels the $d_km$ term and proves

\[
\dim\mathcal X_{\mathbf B}[K]\langle\Psi\rangle\le d_Fm+\ell+a-q-s.
\]

For the vector-matroid estimate, let $r(S)=\dim_{\mathbb Q}\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb Q}(S)$. Since $S\subseteq\Psi_2$, one has $q\ge r(S)$, hence

\[
a-q\le a-r(S).
\]

Nullity is monotone under inclusion in a matroid, and $S\subseteq\Phi^+$, so

\[
a-r(S)\le \#\Phi^+-\operatorname{rank}(\Phi^+)=N-\ell.
\]

This proves the claimed vector-matroid inequality. It follows that

\[
\dim\mathcal X_{\mathbf B}[K]\langle\Psi\rangle\le d_Fm+N-s,
\]

and subtracting from $d_FN$ gives $d_F(N-m)-N+s$. If $K\ne U$, then $N-m\ge1$, so for $d_F\ge N+1$ this is at least $1+s>0$; at the manuscript threshold $d_F\ge N+2$ it is at least $2+s$.

The equality in the old coarse type-$A_3$ calculation is artificial. That estimate replaced $a-q-s$ by the crude bound $m$, thereby counting every possible $s_\alpha$ while discarding both the rank-$q$ character cuts and the stabilizer term $s$. For $A_3$, $N=6$, $\ell=3$, $m=5$, and $d_F=8$, it gives $(d_F+1)m+\ell=48=d_FN$. The matroid inequality instead gives

\[
\dim\mathcal X_{\mathbf B}[K]\langle\Psi\rangle\le d_Fm+N-s=46-s<48.
\]

Thus the former equality is an artifact of the coarse count, not an equality allowed by the corrected linear equations; the complete BCH and terminal equations may sharpen the inequality further.

# Statement


Let \(F/\mathbb Q_p\) be finite with \([F:\mathbb Q_p]\ge2\) and \(p>2\), let \(G=\operatorname{PGL}_2\), and let \(B\subset G\) be a Borel subgroup. Then the natural morphism of reduced Emerton--Gee stacks
\[
\mathcal X_{B,\mathrm{red}}^{\mathrm{EG}}
\longrightarrow
\mathcal X_{G,\mathrm{red}}^{\mathrm{EG}}
\]
induces a bijection of irreducible components. The same conclusion holds after taking a product with a split central torus.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

Put \(d=[F:\mathbb Q_p]\). If \(d\ge3\), then \(d\ge\#\Phi^++2\) because \(\#\Phi^+=1\) for \(\operatorname{PGL}_2\). COR8-001 therefore gives the component bijection directly.

It remains to treat \(d=2\). F02-017 identifies the irreducible components of the Borel stack with its own rank-one Borel Weil--Deligne labels. Away from the cyclotomic component, a label is an integral torus component. On the cyclotomic component, COR17-011 gives the integral formal crossing
\[
s u=0,
\]
with horizontal label \(V(s)\) and Steinberg label \(V(u)\), and COR17-014 identifies their mod-\(p\) horizontal and Steinberg realizations. For products with torus coordinates, the labels are the maximal branch labels. COR19-001 shows that these integral torus, horizontal, and Steinberg labels persist to the special fiber without coalescing.

COR19-002 now supplies global coverage in degree two: the scheme-theoretic image of its one finite disjoint union of rank-one reductions is the entire reductive stack. In semisimple rank one, the component-generic pieces of those reductions are exactly the Borel labels just described. Hence every irreducible reductive component has a dense image from one of these Borel components.

For each Borel label choose a dense component-generic maximally nonsplit open; on a Steinberg label choose it inside the open \(s\ne0\). COR5-001 gives singleton geometric fibers over the component-generic reductive image. Thus two distinct persistent Borel labels cannot have the same reductive component image: if they did, dense constructible images would meet generically and singleton fibers would identify points lying in disjoint labelled opens. Global coverage gives the converse exhaustivity, so the component map is bijective.

This argument compares labels only within the Borel source and follows their images under the natural Borel-to-reductive morphism. It does not identify or compare unrelated Borel-valued and reductive Weil--Deligne targets. A split central torus contributes the same Cartesian component-label factor on both sides, so taking its product preserves the bijection.

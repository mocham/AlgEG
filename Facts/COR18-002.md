# Statement


Let \(F/\mathbb Q_p\) be finite and let \(\mathbf G\) be the split pinned connected reductive group with connected center in the manuscript's standing setup, with \(p>h_{\mathbf G}\). Let \(\mathbf B\) be its fixed Borel.

If \(\mathbf G\) has type \(F_4\) and \(F\ne\mathbb Q_p\), the natural map from the reduced Borel-valued Emerton--Gee stack to the reduced \(\mathbf G\)-valued stack induces a bijection of irreducible components. Both sides are equidimensional of dimension \(24[F:\mathbb Q_p]\), and their components are indexed by \(\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal W_{\mathbf B})\).

If \(\mathbf G\) has type \(F_4\) and \(F=\mathbb Q_p\), the Borel stack is equidimensional of dimension 24. Its components consist of the usual Weil--Deligne-indexed components and the exceptional components indexed by
\[
\coprod_{j=\mathrm I}^{\mathrm {IV}}\operatorname{Irr}_{24}(S_j).
\]
For split simple \(F_4\), row I has the \(p-1\) labels \((a,1,0,1)\), \(a\bmod p-1\), and rows II--IV have one label each. Thus there are exactly \(p+2\) exceptional Borel components, including exactly 19 at \(p=17\). A split central torus of rank \(z\) multiplies the count by \((p-1)^z\), giving \((p+2)(p-1)^z\), and \(19\cdot16^z\) at \(p=17\). The reduced reductive stack is equidimensional of dimension 24 and has only the usual components indexed by \(\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal W_{\mathbf B})\). Hence the exact Borel-to-reductive excess is the displayed exceptional count; none of those exceptional Borel components creates a reductive component.

For arbitrary \(\mathbf G\), if
\[
[F:\mathbb Q_p]>\dim(\mathbf G)/2,
\]
the natural Borel-to-reductive map induces a bijection of irreducible components. The reductive stack is equidimensional of dimension \([F:\mathbb Q_p]\#\Phi^+\), and its components are indexed by \(\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal W_{\mathbf B})\).

## Expanded self-contained proof

COR18-001 proves the complete corrected \(F_4\) Borel classification and the exact exceptional count. COR19-002 proves global finite scheme-image coverage and upgrades the corrected dimension and rotation inputs to exhaustivity by full maximally nonsplit Borel images for every reductive component.

For type \(F_4\) with \(F\ne\mathbb Q_p\), COR8-001 gives the Borel-to-reductive component bijection and equidimensionality. For \(F=\mathbb Q_p\), COR10-001 classifies all usual and Serre-indexed exceptional images, and F05-022 gives the unconditional reductive theorem. Componentwise rotation places every exceptional image in a usual component closure, while COR19-002 proves that the usual images are exhaustive. Therefore the reductive stack has only the usual components, and the Borel excess is exactly \(p+2\) for split simple \(F_4\), with the stated central-torus multiplier.

Now let \(\mathbf G\) be arbitrary and put \(d=[F:\mathbb Q_p]\), \(N=\#\Phi^+\), and \(r=\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf G)\), so \(\dim\mathbf G=2N+r\). If the semisimple rank is at least two, then \(r\ge2\), and the integer inequality \(d>\dim\mathbf G/2=N+r/2\) gives \(d\ge N+2\). COR15-004 supplies the large-degree Borel classification and COR8-001 gives the asserted component bijection and equidimensionality.

If the semisimple rank is one, the root-datum classification used in MAIN-001 reduces the split connected-center group to the \(\operatorname{PGL}_2\) or \(\operatorname{GL}_2\) rank-one factor together with a split central torus. The numerical hypothesis forces \(d\ge2\), so COR17-010 gives the rank-one component bijection, and its split-central-torus clause gives the product statement. For a torus the comparison is tautological. These cases prove the arbitrary-group clause. The integral component-label specialization needed in the corrected proof spine is supplied by COR19-001 through the Verified component-count inputs used by COR8-001.

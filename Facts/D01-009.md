# Statement


Let $k$ be a field of characteristic different from $2$, let $(V,\omega)$ be a nonzero finite-dimensional vector space with a nondegenerate alternating form, and let $a_{12},a_{13},a_{23}\in k^\times$. There do not exist three ordered bases with basis matrices $P,Q,R$ such that $$a_{12}P^{\mathsf T}\omega Q=I,\qquad a_{13}P^{\mathsf T}\omega R=I,\qquad a_{23}Q^{\mathsf T}\omega R=I.$$ Consequently, bases transported around an odd cycle of scalar multiples of one alternating perfect pairing cannot normalize every edge-pairing matrix to the identity.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

The first two equalities and invertibility of $P^{\mathsf T}\omega$ imply $a_{12}Q=a_{13}R$, so $Q=(a_{13}/a_{12})R$. The third left-hand side is therefore a nonzero scalar multiple of $R^{\mathsf T}\omega R$, which is alternating and has zero diagonal. It cannot equal the identity matrix, whose diagonal entries are one.

## Post-proof remarks

No internal issues. From the first two equations, invertibility of P^T omega gives Q=(a13/a12)R; the third matrix is therefore a nonzero scalar multiple of R^T omega R, which is alternating with zero diagonal and cannot be the identity in positive dimension. For a longer odd cycle, applying the normalized-edge relation twice makes every second basis a nonzero scalar multiple of the preceding same-parity basis, so the closing odd edge has the same alternating obstruction. All scalar and characteristic hypotheses are used, and there are no dependencies.

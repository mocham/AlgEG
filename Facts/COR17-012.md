# Statement


Let \(F/\mathbb Q_p\) be finite and \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_p\). Let \(\mathbf G\) be a split connected reductive group with maximal torus \(\mathbf T\), let \(\mathbf M\subsetneq\mathbf G\) be a Levi subgroup containing \(\mathbf T\), and write \(\Phi_{\mathbf M}\) for the roots of \(\mathbf M\). Let \(w\in N_{\mathbf M}(\mathbf T)\) have elliptic Weyl image, and fix an elliptic representation
\[
\bar\rho^{\mathrm{ss}}:G_F\longrightarrow \mathbf T(k)\langle w\rangle
\]
whose inertia contains an element \(s\) regular semisimple in \(\mathbf M\), so \(\beta(s)\ne1\) for every \(\beta\in\Phi_{\mathbf M}\).

For a \(w\)-relative root \(\boldsymbol\alpha=\langle w\rangle\alpha\), let \(U_{\boldsymbol\alpha}\) be the product of its root factors in the corresponding relative-height associated graded quotient, with the \(G_F\)-action induced by \(\bar\rho^{\mathrm{ss}}\). If
\[
H^2(G_F,U_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(k))\ne0
\quad\text{and}\quad
H^2(G_F,U_{\boldsymbol\alpha'}(k))\ne0,
\]
then no two roots in \(S=\boldsymbol\alpha\cup\boldsymbol\alpha'\) have a nonzero difference in \(\Phi_{\mathbf M}\); equivalently,
\[
((S-S)\setminus\{0\})\cap\Phi_{\mathbf M}=\varnothing.
\]
For every \(w\)-relative root \(\boldsymbol\beta\) and \(i\in\{0,2\}\), nonvanishing implies
\[
\dim_k H^i(G_F,U_{\boldsymbol\beta}(k))=1.
\]

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

COR2-002 places each orbit product in the commutative relative-height associated graded. If \(m=\#\boldsymbol\beta\) and \(F_m/F\) is the unramified extension of degree \(m\), restriction to \(G_{F_m}\) splits the orbit module into the Frobenius conjugates of one root character \(\chi\).

By local Tate duality, nonzero \(H^2\) forces one conjugate of \(\chi\) to be cyclotomic. Frobenius invariance of the cyclotomic character makes every conjugate cyclotomic. Thus, for two obstructed orbits, the quotient character associated with every difference of roots in their union is trivial on inertia. If a nonzero such difference were a root \(\beta\in\Phi_{\mathbf M}\), then \(\beta(s)=1\), contradicting regular semisimplicity of \(s\).

Frobenius reciprocity identifies \(H^0\) of an orbit induction with \(H^0(G_{F_m},\chi)\), which is zero or one-dimensional. Applying local Tate duality to the Cartier dual gives the same conclusion for \(H^2\).

## Faithful GrobV14 proof

```tex
\begin{proof}
%Let $s$ be a generator of the image of
%$\bar\rho^\SS(I_F)$.
Write $m$ for $\#\Balpha$
and let $F_m/F$ be the unramified extension of degree $m$.
Then as a $\Gal_{F_m}$-module,
$U_{\Balpha}(\bFp)$
is a direct sum of Galois characters
$\chi, \chi^q, \cdots, \chi^{q^{m-1}}$
where $\chi:\Gal_{F_m}\to \bFp^\times$
is the adjoint action on $U_{\alpha^\vee}$.
By local Tate duality \cite[Proposition 5.1.26(1)]{EG23},
\[
H^2(\Gal_F, U_{\Balpha}(\bFp))
\cong H^0(\Gal_F, U_{\Balpha}(\bFp)^\vee(1))^\vee
\]
and if this group is nonzero,
then at least one of
$\{\chi, \chi^q, \cdots, \chi^{q^{m-1}}\}$
must be the cyclotomic character,
and thus they must all be the cyclotomic character.
This implies each quotient
$\chi/\chi^{q^k}$ is the trivial character.
\EDITXIV{If $\Balpha-\Balpha$ contains a nonzero root}
$\beta$
of $\Phi_{\bM}$,
then the action of $\bar\rho^\SS(I_F)$
on the root group $U_{\beta^\vee}$ is trivial,
which contradicts the regular-semisimple inertia assumption in
Setup~\ref{setup:w}.
\EDITXIV{The same argument applies to the union of two obstructed orbits.
For a relative root $\Bbeta$ of size $m$, its root module is induced from
the character of one root group over $F_m$.  Frobenius reciprocity shows
that its $H^0$ is either zero or one-dimensional, and local Tate duality
gives the same conclusion for $H^2$.}
\end{proof}
```

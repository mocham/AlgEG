# Statement


Let $p=5$, $F=\mathbb Q_5$, $k=\overline{\mathbb F}_5$, and $R=k[t^{\pm1}]$. Let $M$ be the constant scalar extension to $R$ of the mod-$5$ cyclotomic character $\overline\varepsilon$, and take $S=R/(t-1)=k$. Then $d_F=d_{k_F}=1$, while the kernel of
\[
H^1_{\mathrm{Herr},+}(M,S)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_1^R(H^2_{\mathrm{Herr}}(M,R),S)
\]
has dimension $3$. Thus the rank-$d_F+d_{k_F}$ conclusion is false for arbitrary constant rank-one families; the calculation instead demonstrates why a valid rank statement must retain the universal unramified-twist-family hypothesis.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

The character $\overline\varepsilon$ is nontrivial and ramified on $G_{\mathbb Q_5}$, so its ordinary $H^0$ vanishes. Local Tate duality gives
\[
H^2(G_F,\overline\varepsilon)\cong H^0(G_F,k)^\vee,
\]
which has dimension one. The local Euler characteristic therefore gives $\dim_k H^1(G_F,\overline\varepsilon)=2$. In the ramified branch of the modified-Herr definition, the added copy of $S\otimes_{\mathbb F_5}k_{F_{\mathrm{cyc}}}=k$ in degree one has no incoming or outgoing differential. Hence $H^1_{\mathrm{Herr},+}(M,S)\cong H^1_{\mathrm{Herr}}(M,S)\oplus k$ has dimension three.

Because $M$ is constant over $R$, scalar extension gives $H^2_{\mathrm{Herr}}(M,R)\cong R$. Thus $\operatorname{Tor}_1^R(R,S)=0$, and the kernel is all of the three-dimensional modified $H^1$. Finally $F_{\mathrm{cyc}}/F$ is totally ramified, so $d_{k_F}=1$ and $d_F+d_{k_F}=2$. The calculation is correct, but the defining character of $M$ is independent of $t$; by contrast, the family in COR1-001 varies through all unramified twists and has torsion, rather than generically nonzero, degree-two cohomology.

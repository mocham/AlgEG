# Statement

Fix a finite extension $K/\mathbb Q_p$ and a coefficient discrete valuation ring $\mathcal O$ with uniformizer $\varpi$. Let $A$ be a Noetherian $\mathcal O/\varpi^a$-algebra such that $A/\varpi$ is countable, and let $M$ be a projective etale $(\varphi,\Gamma)$-module with $A$-coefficients. Then the Herr complex $\mathcal C^\bullet(M)$ is a perfect complex of $A$-modules concentrated in degrees $[0,2]$. If either $B$ is a finite $A$-algebra, or $B$ is a finite-type $A$-algebra and $A$ is finite type over $\mathcal O/\varpi^a$, then there is a natural derived isomorphism $\mathcal C^\bullet(M)\otimes_A^{\mathbb L}B\simeq\mathcal C^\bullet(M\otimes_{\mathbf A_{K,A}}\mathbf A_{K,B})$. In particular, $H^2(\mathcal C^\bullet(M))\otimes_A B\simeq H^2(\mathcal C^\bullet(M\otimes_{\mathbf A_{K,A}}\mathbf A_{K,B}))$.

# Proof or Examples

## Expanded self-contained proof

The terms of the Herr complex are flat over $A$. Nilpotent devissage reduces perfection and base change from $\mathcal O/\varpi^a$ to the residue-field case. In characteristic $p$, choose a $(\varphi,\Gamma)$-stable lattice. On every positive-power lattice, $1-\varphi$ is invertible by the convergent geometric series, so the Herr complex is quasi-isomorphic to a discrete positive truncation. Choose also a $(\psi,\Gamma)$-stable lattice. On a sufficiently negative quotient, $1-\psi$ is invertible, and the topological Herr-to-$\psi$ quasi-isomorphism identifies cohomology with that of the negative lattice. After embedding the projective generic module into a finite free module and clearing bounded $T$-denominators, every term of this lattice complex is a submodule of a finite free $A[[T]]$-module. Its discrete cohomology is therefore finite over $A$. The bounded flat complex with finite cohomology is perfect. For module-finite $B$, $A[[T]]\otimes_A B=B[[T]]$, so the complexes agree directly after base change. In the finite-type case both sides are perfect over $B$; their cone has zero derived fibre at every maximal ideal by the module-finite case over $A$ and over $B$, hence the cone vanishes. The ordinary $H^2$ formula follows from derived base change because there is no cohomology above degree two.

# Statement

Let $\mathcal O$ be a complete discrete valuation ring with fraction field $K$ and uniformizer $\pi$, let $A=\mathcal O[[X_1,\ldots,X_n]]/I$, and replace $A$ by its reduced $\pi$-torsion-free quotient. Let $\widetilde A$ be its finite integral closure. Then the points of $(\operatorname{Spf}A)^{\mathrm{rig}}$ are in functorial bijection with the closed points of $\operatorname{Spec}A[1/\pi]$, with unchanged residue fields, and the morphism $(\operatorname{Spf}\widetilde A)^{\mathrm{rig}}\to(\operatorname{Spf}A)^{\mathrm{rig}}$ is the normal rigid space obtained by integral closure. The irreducible components of $(\operatorname{Spf}A)^{\mathrm{rig}}$ are the images of the connected components of $(\operatorname{Spf}\widetilde A)^{\mathrm{rig}}$. In particular, two points on one rigid irreducible component admit lifts on one connected component of the normal rigid space obtained by integral closure.

# Proof or Examples

## Expanded self-contained proof

The ring $A$ is a quotient of an $\mathcal O$-power-series ring, so $\operatorname{Spf}A$ lies in the formal-scheme class $FS_{\mathcal O}$ used by de Jong and Conrad. De Jong, Lemma 7.1.9, identifies points of its rigid generic fiber with maximal ideals of $A\otimes_{\mathcal O}K=A[1/\pi]$ and identifies their residue fields. The complete power-series quotient is excellent; after reduction its integral closure is therefore finite, and a finite algebra over the complete adic ring is complete. Conrad, Theorem 2.1.3, then identifies the rigid generic fiber of this formal integral closure with the normal rigid space obtained by integral closure. Conrad, Definition 2.2.2, defines each rigid irreducible component as the image of one connected component of that normal rigid space, giving the final lifting assertion.

# Statement


Let \((K,\Psi_0,\Psi_2)\) be a root configuration with \(\Psi_2\subset\Delta_K\), choose \(\rho\in\Psi_0\), and conjugate the universal family by \(\exp(\mu e_{-\rho})\). Over the function field \(F_X\) of its base, make the manuscript's first-order substitutions
\[
t_\delta=1+\varepsilon\lambda_\delta\quad(\delta\in\Psi_0\cap\Delta),
\qquad
x_{\alpha,i}=\varepsilon b_{\alpha,i}\quad(\alpha\in\Psi_0),
\]
leaving the other coordinates unchanged. For \(\alpha\in\Psi_2\) of height greater than one, let \(P_\alpha\) be the constant coefficient in \(\varepsilon\) of the divided equation \(r_\alpha/\varepsilon\), and put
\[
I=(\{P_\alpha\},\ \sum_{\delta\in\Psi_0\cap\Delta}\lambda_\delta-1)
\]
in the resulting polynomial ring \(R\) over \(F_X\). When these equations and variables form a square system, let \(J\) be its Jacobian determinant. Fix a monomial order and write \(G_I\) and \(G_{I+(J)}\) for the reduced Groebner bases.

The manuscript rotational-deformation test reports success exactly when
\[
G_I\ne\{1\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
G_I\ne G_{I+(J)},
\]
equivalently when \(I\ne R\) and \(J\notin I\). This criterion does not by itself imply the existence of a geometric zero at which \(J\) is nonzero unless an additional radicality condition is known.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

A reduced Groebner basis is unique for a fixed monomial order. Thus \(G_I\ne\{1\}\) is equivalent to \(I\ne R\), and \(G_I\ne G_{I+(J)}\) is equivalent to \(I\ne I+(J)\), hence to \(J\notin I\).

The distinction from saturation is real. Over a field of characteristic different from two, take \(I=(x^2)\) and \(J=2x\). Then \(J\notin I\), so the manuscript comparison succeeds, but \(J\in\sqrt I\), every geometric zero has \(J=0\), and saturation by \(J\) is the unit ideal.

## Post-proof remarks

Verified as the exact procedure printed in the manuscript. It is intentionally separated from the stronger saturation criterion implemented in rotation.py. Equality is taken between reduced Groebner bases for a fixed order; the manuscript criterion is ideal membership \(J\notin I\), not radical nonmembership.

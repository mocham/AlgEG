# Statement


Let $F/\mathbb Q_p$ be finite, let $\mathbf G$ be a connected reductive group with connected center, assume $p>h_{\mathbf G}$, let $\mathbf B$ be a Borel subgroup, and let $C$ be an irreducible component of the Borel-valued mod-$p$ Weil-Deligne stack $\mathcal W_{\mathbf B}$. Let $\overline{\dot{\mathcal X}_{\mathbf B}(C)}$ denote the closure in the Borel Emerton-Gee stack of the maximally nonsplit locus lying over $C$. Then there is a regular Borel Hodge type $\underline\lambda_C$ with trivial inertial type such that the special fiber of the corresponding crystalline stack contains $\overline{\dot{\mathcal X}_{\mathbf B}(C)}$.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

Let \(U_2=[U,U]\) and \(\overline U=U/U_2=\prod_{\delta\in\Delta}U_{\delta^\vee}\). F09-005 records Lin25's one component argument: top components of the full Borel fixed-type generic fiber are identified with those of the height-one Weil--Deligne target for \(\overline U\rtimes\mathbf T\).

That height-one group is generated by \(\mathbf T\) and the rank-one factors \(\mathbb G_m\rtimes U_{\delta^\vee}\). Lin25 reduces each factor to the Borel of \(\operatorname{SL}_2\) or \(\operatorname{PGL}_2\). For the projection of \(C\) to each simple-root factor, F05-024 supplies a regular trivial-inertial crystalline type covering the required component. D10-002 combines the simple-root Hodge pairings into one regular \(\mathbf B^-\)-dominant cocharacter \(\underline\lambda_C\). Consequently the height-one crystalline special fiber contains the component indexed by \(C\).

COR17-009 now supplies the entire semistable-to-crystalline step. On the weakly admissible fixed-type locus, the full Borel Weil--Deligne base is isomorphic to the height-one base, and the crystalline locus is its Cartesian pullback along the closed \(N=0\) substack. Therefore the same type \(\underline\lambda_C\) covers the full Borel top component. Taking its flat closure in the tame fixed-type stack of COR17-008 shows that the special fiber contains \(\overline{\dot{\mathcal X}_{\mathbf B}(C)}\).

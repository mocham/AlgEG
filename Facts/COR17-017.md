# Statement


Let \(k\) be a field of characteristic different from two, and consider the perfect degree-one local-Tate pairings between root-coordinate blocks in the split pinned setup. Group tautologically identified root modules into duality blocks under \(M\mapsto M^\vee(1)\), retaining the Chevalley and output-generator units on each oriented root-sum edge.

For a two-element duality orbit \(M\ne M^\vee(1)\), paired bases put every transported perfect pairing in the form \(u_eI_d\). For a self-dual block, graded commutativity makes the pairing alternating, so \(d=2m\), and a symplectic basis puts every transported pairing in the form
\[
u_eH_{2m},
\qquad
H_{2m}=\operatorname{antidiag}(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{m},\underbrace{-1,\ldots,-1}_{m}),
\]
with \(u_e\) a unit. These identity and skew-antidiagonal forms are the blockwise normal forms; a single identity form is not required on a self-dual interaction cycle.

Let \(\Gamma_\Phi\) have vertex set \(\Phi^+\), with \(\beta\) adjacent to \(\gamma\) when \(\beta+\gamma\) is a root. The full graph is a forest exactly in irreducible types \(A_1,A_2,A_3\), and \(B_2=C_2\). In those types all edge forms can be normalized to identity after retaining their explicit units. Every other irreducible crystallographic type contains an odd interaction cycle, so uniform simultaneous identity normalization can fail on an allowed self-dual block. On a bipartite block, exact identity normalization is equivalent to trivial scalar holonomy around every even cycle; a forest satisfies this automatically.

The adjacent-height leading graph used in the recursive Jacobian argument is bipartite in every type. On a self-dual block, if \(i^*=2m+1-i\), then
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\beta,i^*}}
\bigl(x_\beta^{\mathsf T}H_{2m}x_\delta\bigr)
=\pm x_{\delta,i}.
\]
Therefore a hyperbolic slice changes the existing leading Jacobian matrices only by coordinate permutations and unit row or column scalings. Their ranks, monomial minors, bad-prime support, and existing Sage certificates are unchanged. The delicate one-dimensional case has no nonzero perfect alternating self-dual block.

For relative-root groups, form the absolute-root pairing tensors in the relative-height associated graded before descent. Under prime-to-\(p\) unramified descent, descend the tensor and its determinant-line unit, not a chosen symplectic basis. The invariant BCH projection then uses \(x_\beta^{\mathsf T}J_{\beta,\gamma}x_\gamma\) with \(J_{\beta,\gamma}=u_eI_d\) or \(u_eH_{2m}\) on a splitting atlas. Thus nonreduced relative systems require no new root table or Sage certificate.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

Perfect local Tate duality gives the paired-basis identity form on distinct dual blocks. On a self-dual block the cup sign makes the form alternating; characteristic different from two and elementary symplectic linear algebra give the displayed hyperbolic antidiagonal form and force even dimension.

Basis transport along the interaction graph shows that simultaneous identity is equivalent to trivial closed-walk transport. On a bipartite scalar block this is the alternating-product holonomy condition around even cycles. On an odd self-dual cycle, closed-walk transport would force an alternating form to be congruent to a nonzero scalar identity matrix, impossible because the latter has nonzero diagonal. D01-009 records this obstruction.

Root enumeration gives forests in \(A_1,A_2,A_3,B_2\). Minimal odd-cycle carriers in the remaining families are \(A_4\), \(B_3\), \(C_3\), \(D_4\), \(F_4\), and \(G_2\); higher classical and exceptional types contain one of these subsystems. The \(G_2\) triangle is the explicit C01-002 witness. Since \(p>h_\Phi\), characteristic two and every bad Chevalley scalar are excluded.

F03-013 makes the leading adjacent-height graph bipartite. The displayed derivative in a hyperbolic pair proves that every old scalar leading-rank witness survives after a coordinate permutation and a unit scaling. Prime-to-\(p\) descent is exact; keeping the canonical pairing tensor rather than a basis makes the relative construction equivariant.

## Post-proof remarks

Verified corrected replacement for F02-022. Uniform identity normalization is valid only on forests or blocks with trivial holonomy. The robust convention is identity on distinct-dual blocks and the skew-antidiagonal symplectic form on self-dual blocks, with explicit units retained. Large-field estimates use only linear cuts and need no such global normalization; the small-field leading Jacobian certificates are unchanged by hyperbolic slicing.

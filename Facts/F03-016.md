# Statement


Let $\Phi$ be any reduced root system with positive roots $\Phi^+$ and simple roots $\Delta$. Direct an edge $\beta\to\alpha$ when $\alpha-\beta\in\Delta$, and let $\fB(\Phi^+,\Delta,1)$ be the induced bipartite graph on roots of heights one and two. A source-leaf contraction removes a vertex of indegree zero and outdegree one together with its unique out-neighbor. Then there is a finite sequence of source-leaf contractions of $\fB(\Phi^+,\Delta,1)$ whose final graph has only isolated vertices.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

### Faithful original proof

```tex
The height-$2$ roots are the sums of adjacent simple roots. Thus
$\fB(\Phi^+,\Delta,1)$ is the barycentric subdivision of the underlying
Dynkin diagram, which is a tree. Successively remove its leaves.
```

### Complete componentwise argument

Every positive root of height two is the sum of two adjacent simple roots, and the sum of every adjacent pair of simple roots is a positive root. Uniqueness of coordinates in the simple-root basis identifies the height-two roots with the edges of the Dynkin diagram. Hence $\fB(\Phi^+,\Delta,1)$ is its barycentric subdivision, oriented from the original simple-root vertices to the subdividing height-two vertices. For a reducible root system the Dynkin diagram is a forest. In any component containing an edge, choose an original leaf vertex. It has indegree zero and outdegree one, so removing it together with its adjacent subdividing vertex is a source-leaf contraction and corresponds to deleting a leaf edge from the forest. Induction on the number of edges, component by component, leaves only isolated original vertices.

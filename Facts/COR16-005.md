# Statement


Let $k=\overline{\mathbb F}_p$, $R=k[[\varepsilon]]$, $R_n=R/(\varepsilon^{n+1})$, and $\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}_{\mathbb Q_p}$. Let $\mathbf G$ be split of type $F_4$ with $p>h_{\mathbf G}=12$, and fix $\mathbf B=U\rtimes\mathbf T$ and positive roots $\Phi^+$. Let $S,\Psi_0,\Psi_2\subset\Phi^+$ be subsets such that $(S,\Psi_0,\Psi_2)$ equals (I) $(\{0010\},\{0010\},\{0001,0011,0100,0110,0120\})$; (II) $(\{0010\},\{0010\},\{0001,0011,0100,0110,0120,1000\})$; (III) $(\{1000,0010\},\{0010,1000\},\{0001,0011,0100,0110,0120,1100,1110,1120\})$; or (IV) $(\{1000,0001,0011,0010\},\{0001,0010,0011,1000\},\{0100,0110,0111,0120,0121,0122,1100,1110,1111,1120,1121,1122\})$. Set $\Phi_K=\Phi^+\setminus S$, $K=U_{\Phi_K}$, $K_1=K$, $K_{h+1}=[K,K_h]$, $\bar K_h=K_h/K_{h+1}$, $\Delta_K=\{\alpha\in\Phi_K:U_{\alpha^\vee}\subset\bar K_1\}$, $\Psi=(\Psi_0,\Psi_2)$, and $V_K:=U/K_2\cong\prod_{\alpha\in(\Phi^+\setminus\Phi_K)\cup\Delta_K}U_{\alpha^\vee}$. Write $\mathcal X_{L\rtimes\mathbf T}\langle\Psi\rangle$ for the finite-type residual fine locus determined by $(L,\Psi_0,\Psi_2)$ for $L=K$ or $\bar K_1$; its root characters have nonzero degree-two cohomology exactly on $\Psi_2$. Every listed triple satisfies $\Psi_2\subset\Delta_K$. Reduction induces a smooth surjective morphism $q:\mathcal X_{K\rtimes\mathbf T}\langle\Psi\rangle\to\mathcal X_{\bar K_1\rtimes\mathbf T}\langle\Psi\rangle$ whose map on underlying Zariski spaces is open. Negative-root conjugation by a root in $\Psi_0$ preserves $K_2$ on each listed locus and commutes with $q$. For $H=U$ with residual subgroup $H_0=K$, or for $H=V_K$ with residual subgroup $H_0=\bar K_1$, say that a $k$-point $\bar\rho:\Gamma\to(H_0\rtimes\mathbf T)(k)$ has property $\operatorname{Rot}_H$ if there exist $\delta\in\Psi_0$, $\mu\in R$, and a continuous representation $\rho_H:\Gamma\to(H\rtimes\mathbf T)(R)$ such that $\bar\rho=\exp(-\mu e_{-\delta})\rho_H\exp(\mu e_{-\delta})\pmod{\varepsilon}$ and $\rho_H\otimes_R k((\varepsilon))$ does not factor through $N\rtimes\mathbf T$ for any proper $\mathbf T$-stable normal root subgroup $N\subsetneq H_{k((\varepsilon))}$, meaning a proper normal subgroup generated by a $\mathbf T$-stable set of root groups. Then $\exists$ a Zariski-dense substack $\mathcal U_K\subset\mathcal X_{K\rtimes\mathbf T}\langle\Psi\rangle$ such that $\forall\bar\rho\in\mathcal U_K(k)$, $\operatorname{Rot}_U(\bar\rho)$, if and only if $\exists$ a Zariski-dense substack $\mathcal U_1\subset\mathcal X_{\bar K_1\rtimes\mathbf T}\langle\Psi\rangle$ such that $\forall\bar\rho\in\mathcal U_1(k)$, $\operatorname{Rot}_{V_K}(\bar\rho)$. Density in this assertion means Zariski density on the finite-type residual loci, not density in the inverse system $(R_n)_n$.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

**Finite-level morphism and topology.** On a fine locus, $\Psi_2$ records exactly the root characters with nonzero degree-two obstruction. Since $\Psi_2\subset\Delta_K$, every $\alpha\in\Phi_K\setminus\Delta_K$ has $H^2(\Gamma,U_{\alpha^\vee}(k))=0$. Order the higher $K$-root groups compatibly with the descending central filtration. The kernel $K_h/K_{h+1}$ has a finite $\Gamma$-stable filtration whose graded pieces are these root modules, so the long exact cohomology sequences give $H^2(\Gamma,K_h/K_{h+1})=0$. The multiplication-defect cocycle can therefore be killed successively, and every geometric point of the $\bar K_1$ fine locus lifts to the $K$ fine locus. The same calculation is the relative obstruction calculation for $q$. On the explicit destackified affine charts, all equations indexed by $\Psi_2$ have $K$-height one and adjoining the higher-root cocycles is an iterated affine-space torsor. Thus $q$ is smooth and surjective; since the loci are noetherian and $q$ is of finite type, its map on underlying Zariski spaces is open. If $D_1$ is dense in the target, then $q^{-1}(D_1)$ is dense because every nonempty open $O$ in the source has nonempty open image $q(O)$. If $D_K$ is a dense finite-type substack of the source, then $q(D_K)$ meets every nonempty open in the target. Chevalley's theorem makes this image constructible, so componentwise it contains a dense open substack $D_1$, and every geometric point of that $D_1$ has a preimage in $D_K$. These are statements about the residual Zariski spaces, not the $\varepsilon$-adic tower. For the four root sets, the root-string implication $\alpha\in\Phi_{K_2}$ and $\alpha-j\delta\in\Phi^+$ with $\delta\in\Psi_0$ implies $\alpha-j\delta\in\Phi_{K_2}$. Together with $t_\delta=1$ on the fine locus, this proves that negative-root conjugation preserves $K_2$ and that $q$ is equivariant.

**Compatible lifting over $k[[\varepsilon]]$.** Choose $c$ with $K_{c+1}=1$ and put $P_h=(U/K_{h+1})\rtimes\mathbf T$ for $1\le h\le c$. Thus $P_1=V_K\rtimes\mathbf T$, $P_c=\mathbf B$, and for $h\ge2$ the transition $p_h:P_h\to P_{h-1}$ has vector-group kernel $W_h=K_h/K_{h+1}$. Fix a $K$-valued residual point $\bar\rho_K$ and a small witness $(\delta,\mu,\rho_1)$ for $q(\bar\rho_K)$. With $g_0=\exp((\mu\bmod\varepsilon)e_{-\delta})$, equivariance gives a residual lift $\tau_{c,0}=g_0\bar\rho_Kg_0^{-1}:\Gamma\to\mathbf B(k)$ of $\rho_1\bmod\varepsilon$; let $\tau_{h,0}$ be its image in $P_h(k)$. Suppose $\rho_{h-1}:\Gamma\to P_{h-1}(R)$ is constructed. Start with $\rho_{h,0}=\tau_{h,0}$. If $\rho_{h,n}:\Gamma\to P_h(R_n)$ is compatible with $\rho_{h-1}\bmod\varepsilon^{n+1}$, apply D07-001 to $R_{n+1}\twoheadrightarrow R_n$, its square-zero kernel $I_n=\varepsilon^{n+1}R_{n+1}\cong k$, and $1\to W_h\to P_h\to P_{h-1}\to1$. Ordered split-root coordinates give the required point-surjectivity. The action on $W_h\otimes I_n$ factors through the residual representation, and its root filtration plus the preceding vanishings gives $H^2(\Gamma,W_h\otimes I_n)=0$. D07-001 supplies $\rho_{h,n+1}$ reducing exactly to the already chosen $\rho_{h,n}$. Thus, for every $n\ge0$, the transition is explicitly $\rho_{h,n+1}\mapsto\rho_{h,n}$. Since $P_h$ is affine of finite type and $R=\varprojlim_nR_n$, $P_h(R)=\varprojlim_nP_h(R_n)$. The compatible system has a pointwise limit $\rho_h$; it is a homomorphism because all reductions are homomorphisms and is continuous because the reductions to $P_h(R_n)$ form a basis for the $\varepsilon$-adic topology. Repeating this finite construction for $h=2,\ldots,c$ gives a $\mathbf B(R)$-valued lift with residual representation $g_0\bar\rho_Kg_0^{-1}$. The same $\delta$ and $\mu$ therefore satisfy the rotation congruence. No equality $H^2(R)=H^2(k)$ is used.

**Detection of maximal nonsplitting.** Let $L=k((\varepsilon))$ and $\pi:U_L\to(V_K)_L$. If a quotient representation factors through a proper normal root subgroup $Q\subsetneq(V_K)_L$, then the original representation factors through the proper subgroup $\pi^{-1}(Q)$. Conversely, suppose a $\mathbf T$-stable normal root subgroup $N\subset U_L$ has $\pi(N)=V_K$. The $\mathbf T$-weight decomposition then forces $N$ to contain every root group indexed by $(\Phi^+\setminus\Phi_K)\cup\Delta_K$. The root groups indexed by $\Delta_K$ generate $K$: their images generate $K/[K,K]$, and a proper subalgebra of the nilpotent Lie algebra $\operatorname{Lie}K$ cannot surject onto its abelianization. Hence $N$ contains $K$ and all root groups outside $K$, so $N=U_L$. Thus a proper $N$ always has proper image, and a $U_L$-valued representation avoids every proper normal root subgroup if and only if its $V_K$ quotient does.

**The quantified implications.** Given a dense $\mathcal U_1$ with the small property, the dense preimage $q^{-1}(\mathcal U_1)$ has the large property point by point by the compatible construction and normal-subgroup detection. Conversely, from a dense $\mathcal U_K$ with the large property choose a dense open $\mathcal U_1$ inside the constructible image $q(\mathcal U_K)$. For each of its $k$-points choose a geometric preimage in $\mathcal U_K$ and quotient that witness by $K_2$. Equivariance preserves the residual congruence and normal-subgroup detection preserves maximal nonsplitting. This proves both implications with the displayed existential and universal quantifiers.

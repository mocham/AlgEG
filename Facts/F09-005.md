# Statement


Let \(K/\mathbb Q_p\) be finite, and let \((G,B,T,\{X_\alpha\})\) be a pinned quasi-split connected reductive group over \(K\) that splits over a finite extension \(L/K\). Write \({}^LG=\check G\rtimes\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\) and \({}^LB=\check B\rtimes\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\), and suppose \(\operatorname{Res}_{K/\mathbb Q_p}G\) admits a local twisting element \(-\rho\). Let \(\Delta\) be the set of simple positive roots, and let \(\overline U=\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta}U_{\alpha^\vee}\) be the simple-root quotient of the unipotent radical of \(\check B\). Then the composite Borel-valued Weil--Deligne morphism \(\mathcal X_{K,{}^LB}^{L,\rho,\mathrm{rig}}\to\mathrm{WD}_{K,{}^LB}^{L,\mathrm{an}}\to\mathrm{WD}_{K,\overline U\rtimes{}^LT}^{L,\mathrm{an}}\) induces an isomorphism on top Chow groups, the free abelian groups generated by top-dimensional irreducible components.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

Write $W=\mathrm{WD}_{K,\overline U\rtimes{}^LT}^{L,\mathrm{an}}$. For a point $(r,N)$ of $W$, write $N=\sum_iN_i$ along the simple-root groups. The Weil-Deligne relation gives $\operatorname{ad}(r)_i(g)N_i=\lvert g\rvert N_i$, so for every simple root either $N_i=0$ or $\operatorname{ad}(r)_i$ is cyclotomic. The unramified-twist induction of source Lemma 15 shows that the locus where both alternatives hold has dimension $-1$, while $W$ is equidimensional of dimension zero; the analogous intersections for distinct Galois root orbits also have dimension $-1$. Induction on the number of simple roots therefore decomposes the top cycles of $W$ into the locally closed strata $U_S$ indexed by Galois-stable subsets $S\subset\Delta$, where the cyclotomic alternative holds exactly on $S$.

Let $Y^{\mathrm{rig}}$ be the admissible locus in the torus-valued fixed-type stack on which every simple-root adjoint character is either cyclotomic or is not congruent to the cyclotomic character. On this locus the relevant Herr complexes are torsion-free and have vanishing second cohomology. Successive extension along the upper central series of the Borel unipotent radical consequently presents the Borel-valued stack over $Y^{\mathrm{rig}}$ by rigid vector-group torsors. Such torsors preserve irreducible components and top Chow groups, giving the source identifications used in unobs.txt lines 1288-1314.

Every top-dimensional source component meets this good locus. Indeed, induct on quotients of the Borel by normal subgroups of its unipotent radical and choose a central Galois orbit of root groups. If the corresponding adjoint character varies, its image contains an admissible open and source Lemma 14 makes the original component the unique Zariski closure of its intersection with the good locus. If that character is constant, non-cyclotomic, and congruent to cyclotomic, its first cohomology has nonzero torsion; the local Euler characteristic then makes the component dimension strictly smaller than the fixed-type dimension supplied by F09-004, a contradiction. Thus restriction to the good locus induces an isomorphism on source top Chow groups.

Finally restrict over each $U_S$. The diagrams in unobs.txt lines 1374-1409 express the corresponding good source stratum $Y_S$, its torus fixed-type base, and $U_S$ by rigid vector-group or affine-space torsors. The torus fixed-type base and torus Weil-representation base are respectively the rigid generic fiber and rigid analytification of the same algebraic stack, so rigid analytification preserves their irreducible components. Hence $\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathrm{top}}(Y_S)\cong\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathrm{top}}(U_S)$ for every $S$. Each component of $W$ has a dense admissible intersection with a unique $U_S$, and its pullback has a dense admissible intersection with a unique component of $Y_S$; source Lemma 14 again makes both closures unique. Summing over $S$ gives the asserted isomorphism, and the construction is exactly the map induced by the Weil-Deligne morphism.

# Statement


Let $F/\mathbb Q_p$ be finite, put $d_F=[F:\mathbb Q_p]$, and let $\mathbf G$ be a split connected reductive group over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$ with connected center. Fix a Borel subgroup $\mathbf B=U\rtimes\mathbf T$, let $\Delta$ be its set of simple positive roots, and assume $p>h_{\mathbf G}$. For every $\delta\in\Delta$, let $\mathbb G_{m,\delta}$ act on $U_{\delta^\vee}$ through $\delta$, and define
\[
\mathcal W_{\mathbf B}=\mathcal X_{\mathbf T}\times_{\prod_{\delta\in\Delta}\mathcal X_{\mathbb G_{m,\delta}}}\prod_{\delta\in\Delta}\mathcal W_{U_{\delta^\vee}\rtimes\mathbb G_{m,\delta}}.
\]
Then $\mathcal W_{\mathbf B}$ is an algebraic stack of finite type over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$ and is equidimensional of dimension zero. There is a canonical morphism
\[
\operatorname{tr}_1\mathrm{WD}:\operatorname{tr}_1\mathcal X_{\mathbf B}\longrightarrow\mathcal W_{\mathbf B}
\]
whose fibers are irreducible algebraic stacks of constant dimension $d_F\#\Delta$. Consequently, $\operatorname{tr}_1\mathrm{WD}$ induces a bijection on irreducible components.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

The product of the simple-root characters gives a torus morphism
\[
\mathbf T\longrightarrow\prod_{\delta\in\Delta}\mathbb G_{m,\delta}.
\]
Its geometric kernel is the center of $\mathbf G$. The connected-center hypothesis makes this a connected torus kernel, so the torus morphism is smooth and the induced parameter-stack fibers are equidimensional of dimension zero. Apply the rank-one construction F02-016 to every simple root and base-change their product along $\mathcal X_{\mathbf T}$. The resulting fiber product is exactly $\mathcal W_{\mathbf B}$; finite type and dimension zero are preserved by this base change. Put $d_{k_F}=[k_{F_{\mathrm{cyc}}}:\mathbb F_p]$. For each simple root, F02-016 gives a destackified $\mathbb G_a^{d_F+d_{k_F}}$-torsor, while passage back to the representation stack quotients by the $d_{k_F}$-dimensional gauge factor. Thus each rank-one stack fiber is irreducible of dimension $d_F$. Their product is irreducible of dimension $d_F\#\Delta$, and the component bijection follows from surjectivity with irreducible constant-dimensional fibers.

The connected-center qualifier is essential to this argument. For $p=5$ and $\widehat G=\mathrm{SL}_2$, the simple-root map on its maximal torus is $t\mapsto t^2$ and has disconnected kernel $\mu_2$; this example lies outside the corrected scope.

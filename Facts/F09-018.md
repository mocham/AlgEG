# Statement


Let $K/\mathbb Q_p$ be finite, let $l$ be a prime, and let $E/\mathbb Q_l$ have ring of integers $\mathcal O$ and residue field $\mathbb F$. Let $G$ be a smooth affine group scheme over $\mathcal O$ with reductive geometric fibers, and let $\bar\rho:G_K\to G(\mathbb F)$ be residual. Fix an inertial type $\tau$ and, when $l=p$, a $p$-adic Hodge type $\mathbf v$; choose a finite Galois extension $L/K$ on whose inertia $\tau$ is trivial, and let $G\text{-}\mathrm{WD}_E(L/K)$ denote the stack of $G$-valued Weil-Deligne representations trivial on $I_L$. For the natural morphism $\operatorname{Spec}R_{\bar\rho}^{\square,\tau,\mathbf v}[1/l]\to G\text{-}\mathrm{WD}_E(L/K)$, the completed morphism at every closed point of the Jacobson source is formally smooth, and the morphism itself is flat.

## Expanded self-contained proof

### Complete original proof

```tex
The formal smoothness follows from the proofs
of~\cite[Lemma 3.2.1, Proposition 3.3.1]{MR2373358}, which carries over verbatim
to our setting (since the morphism of groupoids from framed deformations to unframed deformations is formally
smooth).
Part~(2) then follows from the fact that formally
smooth morphisms between locally noetherian schemes are flat, which
in turn follows from ~\cite[\S0 Thm.\
19.7.1]{MR0173675}.
```

### Nearby source text

```tex
We continue to fix an inertial type $\tau$ and (if $p=l$) a $p$-adic Hodge type $\mathbf{v}$. For convenience, if $l\ne p$ then for the rest of this section we write $R_{\rhobar}^{\square,\tau,\mathbf{v}}$ for $R_{\rhobar}^{\square,\tau}$; this notational convention allows us to treat the cases $l\ne p$ and $l=p$ simultaneously. We study the generic fibre $R_{\rhobar}^{\square,\tau,\mathbf{v}}[1/l]$ via the morphism
\[ \Spec R_{\rhobar}^{\square,\tau,\mathbf{v}}[1/l]\to G-\WD_E(L/K). \]
```

### Reviewer completion of the flatness step

Put $X=\operatorname{Spec}R_{\bar\rho}^{\square,\tau,\mathbf v}[1/l]$, $Y=G\text{-}\mathrm{WD}_E(L/K)$, and let $f:X\to Y$ be the displayed morphism. By D03-001, the completed morphism at every closed point $x\in X$ is formally smooth. Choose a smooth scheme presentation $V\to Y$, a point $v\in V$ over $f(x)$ after a harmless residue-field extension, and a point $u$ of $U=X\times_YV$ over $(x,v)$. The induced map of completed Noetherian local rings
\[
\widehat{\mathcal O}_{V,v}\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathcal O}_{U,u}
\]
is formally smooth and therefore flat. Since $\mathcal O_{V,v}\to\widehat{\mathcal O}_{V,v}$ is flat, the completion $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{U,u}$ is flat over $\mathcal O_{V,v}$. The completion map $\mathcal O_{U,u}\to\widehat{\mathcal O}_{U,u}$ is faithfully flat, so faithfully flat descent of flatness shows that $\mathcal O_{U,u}$ is flat over $\mathcal O_{V,v}$. Thus $f$ is flat at every closed point of $X$.

Both $X$ and $Y$ are locally of finite type over $E$, so $f$ is locally of finite presentation and its flat locus is open. The source $X$ is Jacobson. If the complement of the flat locus were nonempty, that closed subset would contain a closed point, contradicting the preceding paragraph. Hence $f$ is flat everywhere.

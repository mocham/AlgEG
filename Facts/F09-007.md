# Statement

Let X be an integral good algebraic space with a dimension function, and let Z be an irreducible component of an effective Cartier divisor on X. Then Z has codimension one and dim(Z)=dim(X)-1.

# Proof or Examples

## Expanded self-contained proof

Let $D\subset X$ be the effective Cartier divisor and let $\xi$ be the generic point of the irreducible component $Z$. By Stacks Project, Tag 0EPZ, choose an etale neighbourhood $(U,u)\to(X,\xi)$ with $U=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ such that $D\times_XU=V(f)$ for a nonzerodivisor $f\in A$; moreover, $u$ is a generic point of $V(f)$. Thus in the local ring $R=\mathcal O_{U,u}$ the maximal ideal is minimal over the principal ideal $(f)$. Krull principal ideal theorem gives $\dim R\leq 1$. Since $f$ remains a nonzerodivisor in $R$, the maximal ideal cannot be a minimal prime of $R$, so $\dim R\geq 1$. Hence $\dim R=1$. Etale localization preserves the relevant specialization codimension, so $\xi$ is a codimension-one point of $X$ and $Z$ is a prime divisor. Finally, the defining property of the given dimension function along an immediate specialization gives $\dim(Z)=\dim(X)-1$ (equivalently, this is part (1) of Tag 0EPZ applied to the prime divisor $Z$).

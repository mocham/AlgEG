# Statement


Let $f:\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y$ be a morphism of categories fibered in groupoids that is representable by algebraic spaces. For every scheme $T$ and every object $x\in\mathcal X(T)$, the induced morphism of automorphism sheaves $\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{\mathcal X}(x)\to\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{\mathcal Y}(f(x))$ is a monomorphism. In particular, it is injective on automorphism groups over every field. Consequently, for a homomorphism of group schemes $\phi:H_1\to H_2$, the induced map of classifying stacks $BH_1\to BH_2$ cannot be representable if $\ker(\phi)$ is nontrivial.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

Base change $f$ along the morphism $T\to\mathcal Y$ classified by $f(x)$. By representability, the fiber product is an algebraic space, hence a sheaf of sets and not a groupoid with nontrivial automorphisms after any further base change on $T$. The kernel sheaf of $\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}(x)\to\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}(f(x))$ is exactly the automorphism sheaf of the induced object in this fiber product, so it is trivial. For $BH_1\to BH_2$, the stabilizer morphism at the trivial torsor is $\phi$, and the conclusion follows.

Hence every stabilizer map of a representable morphism is a monomorphism. At the trivial torsor, the stabilizer map for BH1 to BH2 is phi itself, so a nontrivial scheme-theoretic kernel precludes representability.

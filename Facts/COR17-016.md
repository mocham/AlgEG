# Statement


Let \(k\) be a field and let rotation.py construct, from a specialized root record \((\Psi_0,\Psi_2)\) and a rotation root \(\rho\in\Psi_0\), its square polynomial system
\[
f_0=1-\sum_{\delta\in\Psi_0,\ \operatorname{Ht}(\delta)=1}\lambda_\delta,
\qquad
\{f_\alpha:\alpha\in\Psi_2,\ \operatorname{Ht}(\alpha)\ge2\}
\]
in the variables \(\mu\), the \(\lambda_\delta\), and the two first-order variables attached to every root in \(\Psi_0\). Here the coefficients along a \(\rho\)-root string are obtained from the Chevalley recurrence implemented in rotation.py. Put \(I=(f_0,\{f_\alpha\})\) and let \(J\) be the determinant of the square Jacobian matrix.

The implemented saturated rotational-deformation test reports success at this specialization exactly when
\[
I\ne R
\quad\text{and}\quad
I:J^\infty\ne R.
\]
Equivalently, after some field extension there is a common zero of the equations at which \(J\ne0\). Such a point satisfies the invertible-Jacobian hypothesis of F05-018. The finite-field certificate search in rotation.py reports configuration-level success only when it finds a specialization satisfying this criterion.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

The implementation first rejects the unit equation ideal, computes the Jacobian determinant for a square system, saturates by that determinant, and accepts only when the saturated ideal remains proper. D01-001 identifies proper Jacobian saturation with existence of a geometric common zero outside \(V(J)\). F05-018 then supplies the formal lifting consequence at such a point.

Since \(I:J^\infty\ne R\) is equivalent to \(J\notin\sqrt I\), this criterion implies the manuscript condition \(J\notin I\), but the converse fails, as shown by \(I=(x^2)\), \(J=2x\). It is therefore a distinct, stronger test rather than an implementation of the manuscript's literal Groebner-basis comparison.

# Statement


Let $p=5$, $F=\mathbb Q_5$, $k=\overline{\mathbb F}_5$, and let $\mathbf G=\mathrm{PGL}_{3,k}$ have its standard pinning, simple positive roots $\delta_1\prec\delta_2$, and highest root $\gamma=\delta_1+\delta_2$. Take $K=U$, define its $K$-simple-root set by $\Delta_K:=\{\delta_1,\delta_2\}$, and note that $\operatorname{Ht}_K(\gamma)=2$. For the fine pair $\Psi_0=\varnothing$, $\Psi_2=\{\gamma\}$ at $t_\gamma=1$, the literal ordered coefficient of $x_{\delta_1,1}x_{\delta_2,1}$ is $\frac12(N_{\delta_1,\delta_2}+N_{\delta_2,\delta_1})=0$. If the unordered decomposition of $\gamma$ is instead recorded once with orientation $(\delta_1,\delta_2)$, then the standard pinning gives coefficient $N_{\delta_1,\delta_2}=1$. The resulting quadratic monomial is $x_{\delta_1,1}x_{\delta_2,1}$, equal to the quadratic term in the direct universal equation $r_\gamma=s_\gamma(t_1t_2-1)+x_{\delta_1,1}x_{\delta_2,1}$.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

Choose the simple-root fibers $\omega^2$ and $\omega^{-1}$ at $t_1=t_2=1$. They are nontrivial and non-cyclotomic, while their product is $\omega$, so local Tate duality gives the fine pair $\Psi_0=\varnothing$ and $\Psi_2=\{\gamma\}$. The literal ordered notation produces
\[
\frac12\bigl(N_{\delta_1,\delta_2}+N_{\delta_2,\delta_1}\bigr)(\vec x_{\delta_1}\mathbin\cdot\vec x_{\delta_2})=0
\]
because $N_{\delta_2,\delta_1}=-N_{\delta_1,\delta_2}$. This calculation correctly diagnoses the missing orientation sign.

The quadratic BCH term, however, is the half-sum of the two ordered cochain brackets. Interchanging the degree-one cochains changes the cup-product sign as well as the Chevalley sign, so those two brackets agree in the root-$\gamma$ obstruction. Recording their half-sum once with the orientation $\delta_1\prec\delta_2$ gives $N_{\delta_1,\delta_2}\vec x_{\delta_1}\cdot\vec x_{\delta_2}$. For the standard pinning and the specialization with $x_{\delta_1,1}=x_{\delta_2,1}=1$ and all other additive coordinates zero, this is $1$. The direct universal chart gives the same value. Thus the example distinguishes incoherent ordered notation from the corrected oriented formula rather than contradicting the latter.

# Statement


Let $\Phi$ be a simple reduced root system. For every height $h$, repeatedly contract a degree-one source vertex together with its unique adjacent target in the complete adjacent-height graph $\mathcal B(\Phi^+,\Delta,h)$, and discard isolated vertices. Up to isolated vertices, the remaining graph is one of
\[
\emptyset,\quad \vec C_6,\quad
\overrightarrow{C_6\cup_{K_1}P_3},\quad
\overrightarrow{C_6\cup_{K_2}C_6},\quad
\overrightarrow{C_6\cup_{K_2}C_6\cup_{K_1}P_3},\quad
\overrightarrow{C_6\cup_{K_2}C_6\cup_{K_2}C_6}.
\]
Here $P_3$ is the directed path $\circ\leftarrow\circ\to\circ$. For $\Phi=D_n$, the third graph extends to the family
\[
\overrightarrow{C_6\cup_{K_1}P_{2m-1}}
\]
at
\[
h=2m,\qquad 1\le m\le\left\lfloor\frac{n-2}{2}\right\rfloor,
\]
and the graph contracts to the empty graph at every other height. The path $P_{2m-1}$ is alternating and directed from each source to its neighboring targets; for $m=1$ the third graph means $\vec C_6$.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

Write $h=2m$ and $a=n-2m$. The terminal graph $K_{n,m}$ has sources
\[
A_r=P_{a+r,n-r}\ (0\le r<m),\qquad B=M_{a,n},\qquad C=M_{a-1,n-1},
\]
and targets
\[
Q_r=P_{a-1+r,n-r}\ (0\le r\le m),\qquad R=M_{a-1,n}.
\]
Its edges are
\[
A_r\longrightarrow Q_r,Q_{r+1},\qquad B\longrightarrow R,Q_1,\qquad C\longrightarrow R,Q_0.
\]
The six vertices $A_0,B,C,Q_0,Q_1,R$ form $\vec C_6$, and $Q_1-A_1-Q_2-\cdots-A_{m-1}-Q_m$ is the attached $\vec P_{2m-1}$. This proves the given isomorphism and gives $m+2$ sources, $m+2$ targets, and $2m+4$ edges.

For completeness, the standard positive-root formulas give $\operatorname{ht}(M_{ij})=j-i$ and $\operatorname{ht}(P_{ij})=2n-i-j$. Among the roots of height $h\le n-2$, the roots $M_{i,i+h}$ form a chain. Starting at $M_{1,1+h}$, successive degree-one source vertex contractions remove that chain from left to right and leave only $B,C,R$ and their two edges into the plus-root part. If $h=2m$, the remaining plus roots are exactly the $A_r$ and $Q_r$ above; every source then has degree two, so the given graph is terminal. If $h=2m+1$, the last plus source has degree one. Contracting from that endpoint peels the plus path backwards, after which $B$ and $C$ contract against $R$ and the remaining graph is empty. At heights $h\ge n-1$, the minus-root chain has ended and the graphs on the remaining plus roots at those heights are paths with a source endpoint, so the same peeling gives the empty remaining graph. These cases exhaust all positive roots and all heights.

The description depends only on $m$ up to adding leading zero coordinates when the rank increases, proving stability. In particular, $n=8,m=3$ gives exactly
```text
00011211 -> 00012211, 00111211
00111111 -> 00111211, 01111111
01111101 -> 01111111, 11111101
01111110 -> 01111111, 11111110
11111100 -> 11111101, 11111110
```
so the D8 graph at height six is the $\vec C_6\cup_{K_1}\vec P_5$ table row, not a counterexample. The correction verifier reuses the immutable AlgEG graph and contraction routines. It checks every graph on two adjacent heights in $D_4$ through $D_{32}$, all 928 adjacent heights, and finds precisely the 225 remaining graphs predicted by the formula. The finite computation confirms the symbolic classification but is not used to extrapolate it.

For the remaining irreducible types, the same contraction algorithm is finite. In types $A_n,B_n,C_n$, and $G_2$, repeated endpoint contractions remove every component. For $F_4,E_6,E_7$, and $E_8$, enumerate the positive roots from the Cartan matrix, partition them by height, construct every edge $\beta\to\alpha$ with $\alpha-\beta\in\Delta$, and repeat the prescribed degree-one source contractions until none remain. The authorized root-poset computation checks each finite list and returns only the five nonempty graph forms displayed in the statement; at height $9$ in $E_8$, the terminal form is $\vec C_6\cup_{K_2}\vec C_6\cup_{K_1}\vec P_3$. F03-016 proves the height-one case uniformly, while the explicit $M_{ij},P_{ij}$ argument above proves the complete Type D family without extrapolation from finite rank.

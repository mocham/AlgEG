# Statement


Let $p=5$, $F=\mathbb Q_5$, $\mathbf G=\mathrm{GL}_3$, $\mathbf T$ the diagonal torus, $a=e_1-e_2$, $b=e_2-e_3$, and $\mathbf M=\mathrm{GL}_2\times\mathrm{GL}_1$ the proper Levi for $a$. Let $w$ be the order-two permutation matrix swapping the first two basis vectors, let $K/F$ be the unramified quadratic extension, and choose a Frobenius lift $\sigma$ with $\chi_{\mathrm{LT}}(\sigma^2)=1$. Put $\lambda(z)=\operatorname{diag}(z^{-2},z^{-1},1)$, and define $N:G_F\to\mathbf T(\overline{\mathbb Z}_5)\langle w\rangle$ by $N(\sigma)=w$ and
\[
N(g)=\lambda(\chi_{\mathrm{LT}}(g))\,w\lambda(\chi_{\mathrm{LT}}(\sigma^{-1}g\sigma))w^{-1}\qquad(g\in G_K).
\]
For $\delta=b$, the orbit is $\boldsymbol\delta=\{b,a+b\}$. On $G_K$, the character of the factor $U_b$ is $\psi=\chi_0^{-1}\chi_1^{-2}$, where $\chi_0=\chi_{\mathrm{LT}}$ and $\chi_1(g)=\chi_{\mathrm{LT}}(\sigma^{-1}g\sigma)$. Its two labeled Hodge-Tate weights are $(1,2)$ in the cited convention, whereas the cyclotomic character has weights $(-1,-1)$. Hence $H^2(G_K,\overline{\mathbb Q}_5(\psi))=0$, and Shapiro plus scalar inversion shows that $H^2(G_F,U_{\boldsymbol\delta}(\overline{\mathbb Z}_5))$ is not a free rank-one $\overline{\mathbb Z}_5$-module. This specific negative-all-simple cocharacter is therefore a bad choice for forcing nonzero second cohomology on the root orbit.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

All ambient restrictions in the example hold: $\mathrm{GL}_3$ has connected center, $5>h_{A_2}=3$, the Weyl image of $w=s_a$ is elliptic in the $A_1$ Weyl group of $\mathbf M$, and $b\notin\Delta_{\mathbf M}$. The chosen cocharacter satisfies
\[
\langle b,\lambda\rangle=-1,\qquad w^{-1}b=a+b,\qquad\langle a+b,\lambda\rangle=-2.
\]
For $g\in G_K$, evaluation of the torus norm formula on the root $b$ therefore gives
\[
\psi(g)=b(\lambda(\chi_0(g)))\,b(w\lambda(\chi_1(g))w^{-1})=\chi_0(g)^{-1}\chi_1(g)^{-2}.
\]
A Frobenius lift with $\chi_{\mathrm{LT}}(\sigma^2)=1$ exists because the Lubin-Tate extension is totally ramified. For this choice the norm character satisfies the conjugation relation and sends $\sigma^2$ to $w^2=1$, so the displayed formula is presentation-independent in this instance.

The cited Lubin-Tate calculation gives labeled weights $(-1,0)$ for $\chi_0$ and $(0,-1)$ for $\chi_1$. Thus $\psi$ has weights $(1,2)$. Under the opposite reciprocity convention both signs reverse, but they remain unequal, while every cyclotomic twist has equal labeled weights. Hence $\psi\ne\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}$. Characteristic-zero local Tate duality gives
\[
H^2(G_K,\overline{\mathbb Q}_5(\psi))\cong H^0(G_K,\overline{\mathbb Q}_5(\psi^{-1}\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}))^\vee=0.
\]
The two root factors are permuted transitively by $G_F$ with stabilizer $G_K$, so $U_{\boldsymbol\delta}$ is the induced rank-one module and Shapiro identifies its degree-two cohomology with the displayed group over $G_K$. The representation descends to a finite coefficient extension, whose continuous local-cohomology complex is perfect and commutes with inverting $5$ and then extending scalars. If the integral degree-two group were free of rank one over $\overline{\mathbb Z}_5$, its scalar inversion would be $\overline{\mathbb Q}_5$, contradicting the zero calculation.

The calculation concerns only the displayed cocharacter. It proves that unequal Lubin-Tate exponents do not produce the desired cyclotomic stabilizer character; it does not rule out replacing the cocharacter or the torus-valued character by a construction whose orbit stabilizers are cyclotomic.

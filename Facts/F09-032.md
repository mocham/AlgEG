# Statement

Let $X_1$ and $X_2$ be reduced closed subschemes of a scheme, and let $X$ be their scheme-theoretic union. Then $X$ is reduced.

# Proof or Examples

## Expanded self-contained proof

### Expanded proof

Reducedness and scheme-theoretic unions can be checked on affine opens of the given scheme. On an affine open $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$, write $X_i\cap U=\operatorname{Spec}(A/I_i)$. Since $X_i$ is reduced, $I_i$ is radical. The scheme-theoretic union is cut out by $I_1\cap I_2$. If $a^n\in I_1\cap I_2$, then $a\in I_1$ and $a\in I_2$ because both ideals are radical, so $a\in I_1\cap I_2$. Thus $I_1\cap I_2$ is radical and $A/(I_1\cap I_2)$ is reduced. Equivalently, the canonical injection

$$A/(I_1\cap I_2)\hookrightarrow A/I_1\times A/I_2$$

embeds the union’s coordinate ring into a product of reduced rings.

### Faithful full quotation

```text
Lemma 13. (1) The scheme-theoretic union of reduced schemes is reduced.

Proof. (1) Say Spec A is the scheme-theoretic union of Spec A1 and Spec A2. Then Spec A is by definition the scheme-theoretic image of Spec A1 x A2 in the ambient scheme. By [Stacks, Tag 056A], A -> A1 x A2 is injective, and thus A is reduced.
```

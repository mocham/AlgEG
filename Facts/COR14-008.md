# Statement


Retain the split pinned Borel and recursive modified-Herr chart setup with coefficient ring \(R\). For a positive root \(\alpha\) whose root-character component meets the Steinberg crossing, put \(u_\alpha=t_\alpha-1\), choose a generator \(b_{\alpha,\mathrm{std}}\) of
\[
H^2_{\mathrm{Herr}}(U_{\alpha^\vee})\cong R/(u_\alpha),
\]
and choose \(c_{\alpha,\mathrm{std}}\) with
\[
d^1_{\mathrm{Herr}}c_{\alpha,\mathrm{std}}
=u_\alpha b_{\alpha,\mathrm{std}}.
\]
For every degree-two obstruction cocycle \(B_{\alpha,\lambda}\) produced recursively from lower-root gauge cochains, its cohomology class determines a canonical residue
\[
\bar a_{\alpha,\lambda}\in R/(u_\alpha),
\qquad
[B_{\alpha,\lambda}]
=\bar a_{\alpha,\lambda}[b_{\alpha,\mathrm{std}}].
\]
Choose any lift \(a_{\alpha,\lambda}\in R\) and a primitive \(c_{\alpha,\lambda}\) satisfying
\[
B_{\alpha,\lambda}
=a_{\alpha,\lambda}b_{\alpha,\mathrm{std}}
-d^1_{\mathrm{Herr}}c_{\alpha,\lambda}.
\]
If the root component does not meet the Steinberg crossing, \(H^2=0\) and one simply chooses a primitive of \(-B_{\alpha,\lambda}\). Iterating in root-height order gives finite recursive gauge-cochain sets. Only the residue classes \(\bar a_{\alpha,\lambda}\) are canonical; neither their lifts nor the corresponding primitives are asserted to be unique.

More precisely, suppose one root-stage equation and universal cochain contain
\[
r_\alpha=s_\alpha u_\alpha+\sum_j a_jm_j,
\qquad
z_\alpha=\sum_jm_jc_j+s_\alpha c_{\alpha,\mathrm{std}},
\]
where each \(m_j\) uses only lower-root coordinates. If
\[
a'_j=a_j+u_\alpha q_j,
\qquad
c'_j=c_j+q_jc_{\alpha,\mathrm{std}},
\qquad
Q=\sum_jq_jm_j,
\]
then the triangular substitution \(s_\alpha\mapsto s_\alpha-Q\) identifies the two universal cochains and the two root-stage equations. Composing in root-height order gives a triangular isomorphism between charts obtained from any two systems of coefficient lifts.

The same conclusion applies to the coefficient of the complete BCH obstruction. On a fine locus where the root equation is imposed, \(u_\alpha=0\) is already imposed, so changing a lift by \(u_\alpha q\) does not change the fine ideal or its leading root-coordinate Jacobian block. Thus no recursive equation, chart, dimension calculation, or component argument requires literal uniqueness in \(R\).

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

D04-002 identifies the top Herr cohomology at a Steinberg crossing with the cyclic module \(R/(u_\alpha)\), and F02-019 supplies the standard boundary relation. Therefore every obstruction class has a unique residue coefficient and every lift of that residue admits the displayed primitive. Off the crossing, vanishing of \(H^2\) gives a primitive directly. Finiteness follows because the positive root system and every ordered decomposition of a root are finite.

For the comparison of choices, compute
\[
a'_jb_{\alpha,\mathrm{std}}-d(c'_j)
=a_jb_{\alpha,\mathrm{std}}-d(c_j),
\]
using \(d(c_{\alpha,\mathrm{std}})=u_\alpha b_{\alpha,\mathrm{std}}\). Substitution of \(s_\alpha-Q\) then cancels the added terms in both \(z_\alpha\) and \(r_\alpha\). Since \(Q\) involves only lower-root coordinates, these automorphisms compose triangularly. D09-003 gives the same calculation for the total complete-BCH coefficient. Modulo the fine torus equation \(u_\alpha=0\), the two coefficient lifts are literally equal.

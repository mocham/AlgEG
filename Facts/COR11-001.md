# Statement


Let $F/\mathbb Q_p$ be finite, put $d_F=[F:\mathbb Q_p]$ and $d_{k_F}=[k_{F_{\mathrm{cyc}}}:\mathbb F_p]$, and let $\mathbf B=U\rtimes\mathbf T$ be the Borel subgroup of a split pinned reductive group over $k=\overline{\mathbb F}_p$, with positive roots $\Phi^+$ and simple roots $\Delta$. Let $K\subset U$ be a $\mathbf T$-stable normal subgroup with root set $\Phi_K$, $K$-height $\operatorname{Ht}_K$, and $K$-simple roots $\Delta_K$. If $\alpha=\sum_{\delta\in\Delta}m_\delta\delta$, put $t_\alpha=\prod_{\delta\in\Delta}t_\delta^{m_\delta}$. Fix subsets $\Psi_0,\Psi_2\subseteq\Phi^+$ such that $\alpha,\beta\in\Psi_2$ and $\alpha-\beta\in\Phi^+$ imply $\alpha-\beta\in\Psi_0$, while $\alpha\in\Psi_2$, $\beta\in\Psi_0$, and $\alpha+\beta\in\Phi^+$ imply $\alpha+\beta\in\Psi_2$; write $\Psi=(\Psi_0,\Psi_2)$. Put
\[
R=k\left[\begin{matrix}x_{\alpha,1},\ldots,x_{\alpha,d_F+d_{k_F}},s_\alpha&:&\alpha\in\Phi_K\\ t_\delta^{\pm1}&:&\delta\in\Delta\end{matrix}\right].
\]
Give every $x_{\alpha,i}$ and $s_\alpha$ polynomial degree one and every $t_\delta^{\pm1}$ degree zero. Define the common linear ideal
\[
L_\Psi=\bigl(x_{\alpha,d_F+d_{k_F}}:\alpha\notin\Psi_0;\ s_\alpha:\alpha\notin\Psi_2;\ t_\alpha-1:\alpha\in\Psi_0\cup\Psi_2\bigr).
\]
For each $\alpha\in\Psi_2$ with $\operatorname{Ht}_K(\alpha)>1$, write the actual gauge obstruction uniquely by polynomial degree as
\[
r_\alpha=s_\alpha(t_\alpha-1)+q_\alpha+h_\alpha,
\]
where $q_\alpha$ is its complete homogeneous quadratic part and every monomial of $h_\alpha$ has degree at least three. The corrected fine model and corrected cone model are
\[
X_{K,\mathbf T}\langle\Psi\rangle=\operatorname{Spec}R/\bigl(L_\Psi+(r_\alpha)_\alpha\bigr),
\qquad
X_{K,\mathbf T}\langle\Psi\rangle^{\mathrm{cone}}=\operatorname{Spec}R/\bigl(L_\Psi+(s_\alpha(t_\alpha-1)+q_\alpha)_\alpha\bigr).
\]
Equivalently, because $t_\alpha-1\in L_\Psi$ for every obstruction root, the two obstruction ideals modulo $L_\Psi$ are generated respectively by $q_\alpha+h_\alpha$ and $q_\alpha$. Thus the cone construction retains every linear generator, including $x_{\alpha,d_F+d_{k_F}}$ for $\alpha\notin\Psi_0$, and every quadratic obstruction term; it deletes only cubic and higher-degree terms.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

The introductory definition at Grob V13 lines 795-808 says exactly that a cone model is formed by removing cubic and higher-degree terms from the stratum equations. The fine presentation at lines 3050-3110 and the printed cone presentation at lines 3187-3220 use the same ambient gauge-coordinate ring. The printed cone row $x_{\alpha,d_F+d_{k_F}}=0$ is linear, so the introductory definition forces it to be a generator of the fine presentation as well: degree truncation cannot create a new linear equation. The omission of that row from the displayed fine ideal is therefore the unique local transcription error.

The other displayed rows are already common. The equations $s_\alpha=0$ and $t_\alpha-1=0$ are linear in the stated grading and survive unchanged. For an obstruction root, $t_\alpha-1$ is itself in $L_\Psi$, so the term $s_\alpha(t_\alpha-1)$ is redundant on both sides. Splitting the remaining obstruction by polynomial degree leaves its complete quadratic part $q_\alpha$ and removes precisely $h_\alpha$. The oriented highest-lower-height contribution to $q_\alpha$ is the one proved in COR4-002; defining $q_\alpha$ as the complete quadratic part also retains any other quadratic contribution and therefore matches the source's degree-truncation language exactly.

In particular, the corrected definition does not add a terminal-coordinate equation only to the cone and does not delete one only from the fine side. Both models are quotients by the same $L_\Psi$.

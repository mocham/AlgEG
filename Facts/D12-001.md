# Statement


Let $k$ be a field and let $R=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n,u_1^{\pm1},\ldots,u_s^{\pm1}]$ carry an integer weight filtration for which the $u_j$ have weight zero. Let $a_1,\ldots,a_e\in R$ be weight-homogeneous, let $r_1,\ldots,r_m\in R$, and write $f_i=\operatorname{in}_w(r_i)$ for the highest-weight form of $r_i$. Put $I=(a_1,\ldots,a_e,r_1,\ldots,r_m)$ and $J=(a_1,\ldots,a_e,f_1,\ldots,f_m)$. Then
\[
\dim R/J\geq\dim R/I.
\]

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

For every $j$, weight-homogeneity gives $a_j=\operatorname{in}_w(a_j)$, and for every $i$ one has $f_i=\operatorname{in}_w(r_i)$. Hence
\[
J\subseteq\operatorname{in}_w(I).
\]
The weighted Rees construction gives a flat degeneration of $R/I$ to $R/\operatorname{in}_w(I)$; equivalently, after homogenizing with a Rees parameter, the nonzero fibers are isomorphic to $R/I$ and the zero fiber is $R/\operatorname{in}_w(I)$. Localization in the weight-zero variables $u_j$ commutes with this construction. Therefore
\[
\dim R/\operatorname{in}_w(I)=\dim R/I.
\]
The ideal inclusion $J\subseteq\operatorname{in}_w(I)$ induces a surjection $R/J\twoheadrightarrow R/\operatorname{in}_w(I)$, so Krull dimension cannot increase under that quotient. Thus
\[
\dim R/J\geq\dim R/\operatorname{in}_w(I)=\dim R/I.
\]
In particular, this applies when fixed homogeneous equations are retained and each obstruction equation is replaced only by its leading cone form. It does not cover additional equations imposed solely on the cone model.

# Statement


Let $K/\mathbb Q_p$ be finite and let $n>1$ be prime to $p$. Take
$G=H=\mathbb G_m$ and the central isogeny
$i=[n]:\mathbb G_m\to\mathbb G_m$; the induced map of dual L-groups is also
$[n]$. Give both L-groups trivial Hodge type and trivial inertial type, and
let $x$ be the trivial parameter. The stabilizer of $x$ and the stabilizer
of its image are both $\mathbb G_m$, and the induced stabilizer homomorphism
is
\[
\mathbb G_m\xrightarrow{[n]}\mathbb G_m,
\qquad z\longmapsto z^n,
\]
with nontrivial kernel $\mu_n$. Therefore the induced morphism between the
stacks with these Hodge and inertial types is not representable by algebraic
spaces, and in particular is not relatively representable by a finite
morphism.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

### Faithful quotation from the cited paper

```text
(3) If i : G -> H is a central isogeny of reductive groups (with finite kernel), then X_{K,{}^LH}^{tau,lambda} -> X_{K,{}^LG}^{i(tau),i(lambda)} is relatively representable by a finite morphism.

(3) Say X1 and X2 are two objects of X_{K,{}^LH}^{tau,lambda}(Spec A) that map to the same object of X_{K,{}^LG}^{i(tau),i(lambda)}(Spec A), then it makes sense to take their ratio ``X1/X2'', which is a well-defined object of X_{K,Z} where Z is the kernel of H-check -> G-check. Since Z is a multiplicative finite group scheme, X_{K,Z} is the same as the moduli of Weil representations and is finite over Spf Zbar_p.
```

### Counterexample

Fix an integer $n>1$ prime to $p$, take $G=H=\mathbb G_m$ over $K$, and let $i=[n]:\mathbb G_m\to\mathbb G_m$. The dual L-group map is again $[n]:{}^LH\to{}^LG$, with kernel $\mu_n$. Take trivial Hodge and inertial types; the tame-case existence statement in the same paper applies, and the trivial parameter is a point of $\mathcal X_{K,{}^LH}^{\tau,\lambda}$. Because the dual torus is abelian, its conjugation action fixes this parameter, so its automorphism group is $\widehat H=\mathbb G_m$; the image parameter likewise has automorphism group $\widehat G=\mathbb G_m$. The induced stabilizer homomorphism is $[n]$ and has nontrivial kernel $\mu_n$. By D01-006, the stack morphism is not representable by algebraic spaces, hence cannot be relatively representable by a finite morphism.

## Post-proof remarks

The power-isogeny example gives the representability obstruction directly. D01-006 proves that representability by algebraic spaces forces injective stabilizer homomorphisms, whereas $z\mapsto z^n$ has kernel $\mu_n$. The ratio argument in the quoted paper controls isomorphism classes but does not eliminate stabilizer kernels; finiteness of $\mathcal X_{K,\mu_n}$ therefore does not imply representability of the stack morphism.

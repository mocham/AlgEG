# Statement


Let \(\mathcal O\) be a complete discrete valuation ring with generic point \(\eta\) and special point \(s\). Let \(\mathfrak W\) be a Noetherian flat formal stack with finite-type reduction. Assume that it has a finite persistent component-label system: closed \(\mathcal O\)-flat substacks \(\mathfrak W_\lambda\), \(\lambda\in\Lambda\), whose generic and special fibers are irreducible and give all irreducible components of \(\mathfrak W_\eta\) and \(\mathfrak W_s\), with distinct labels in both fibers.

Let \(\mathfrak X\) be \(\mathcal O\)-flat with equidimensional special fiber of dimension \(d\), and let a formal morphism
\[
f:\mathfrak X\longrightarrow\mathfrak W
\]
induce a componentwise-surjective bijection on irreducible components of rigid generic fibers. If every generic source component has dimension \(d\), then there is an injection
\[
\operatorname{Irr}(\mathfrak W_s)
\hookrightarrow
\operatorname{Irr}(\mathfrak X_s).
\]
More precisely, the reduced closure of the generic source component labelled by \(\lambda\) has a special-fiber component dominating \((\mathfrak W_\lambda)_s\), and choices for distinct labels are distinct ambient components.

The following give persistent target labels in the corrected Weil--Deligne setting.

1. A connected component on which top Herr cohomology vanishes is its integral formal torus Weil stack and has one persistent label.
2. If \(A\) is \(\mathcal O\)-flat and integral in both fibers and \(u\) cuts out a prime Cartier divisor in both fibers, then
\[
\operatorname{Spf}A\langle s\rangle/(su)
\]
has the two persistent labels \(V(s)\) and \(V(u)\). They are flat and integral in both fibers and meet properly, so the horizontal and Steinberg branches do not coalesce.
3. For a product of such crossings over a split formal torus, irreducible labels are the maximal closed branch loci obtained by imposing \(s_i=0\) or the character divisor \(u_i=0\), after decomposing simultaneous character fibers into torus translates. If the relevant character-lattice component groups have order prime to \(p\), this component and containment poset is unchanged by reduction.
4. Effective quotient descent by a finite group of order invertible in \(\mathcal O\) replaces the label set by the same orbit set in both fibers.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

Formal specialization is surjective: every special point is the center of a valuation on the rigid generic fiber. Fix \(\lambda\). Componentwise generic surjectivity and specialization therefore show that the special fiber of the reduced source closure maps onto \((\mathfrak W_\lambda)_s\). Some source component dominates that irreducible target. The closure is flat and its special fiber is an effective Cartier divisor, so F09-007 gives dimension \(d\); equidimensionality makes it an ambient component. One ambient component cannot dominate two distinct target components under the common special-fiber morphism, proving injectivity.

For one crossing, \((su)=(s)\cap(u)\) in each fiber. The two prime ideals remain distinct after reduction because \(u\) is a regular prime Cartier equation. For several factors, the closed branch loci form a finite cover; the maximal irreducible members of a finite closed cover are exactly the irreducible components. Character fibers of a split torus with prime-to-\(p\) component groups are finite etale unions of torus translates in both fibers, so their labels and containments persist. Finally, components of a finite quotient are indexed by orbits of components upstairs, and COR16-003 identifies the same quotient groupoid before and after reduction.

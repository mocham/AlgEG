# Statement


Let $p=5$, let $F/\mathbb Q_5$ be the unramified quadratic extension, put $k=\overline{\mathbb F}_5$, and let $\mathbf G=\mathrm{PGL}_{2,k}$ with standard Borel $\mathbf B=U\rtimes\mathbf T$ and sole positive root $\delta$. Choose a semisimple torus character whose root character is $\bar\varepsilon^2$, put $K=U$, and take $\Psi_0=\Psi_2=\varnothing$. Then $d_F=d_{k_F}=2$, the corresponding fine open is nonempty, and the corrected fine and cone coordinate rings are both
\[
B=k[x_{\delta,1},x_{\delta,2},x_{\delta,3},x_{\delta,4},s_\delta,t_\delta^{\pm1}]/(x_{\delta,4},s_\delta)
\cong k[x_{\delta,1},x_{\delta,2},x_{\delta,3},t_\delta^{\pm1}].
\]
They are smooth domains of dimension four, and the fine-open condition $(x_{\delta,1},x_{\delta,2})\ne(0,0)$ does not change that dimension. By contrast, the pair of rings
\[
A=k[x_{\delta,1},x_{\delta,2},x_{\delta,3},x_{\delta,4},s_\delta,t_\delta^{\pm1}]/(s_\delta),\qquad A/(x_{\delta,4})
\]
is not a fine-model/cone-model pair: it deletes the linear equation $x_{\delta,4}=0$ only from the fine side, although truncation through degree two retains every linear equation. Hence its dimension drop from five to four is not a counterexample to cone-to-fine dimension comparison.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

The group $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ is split connected reductive, its trivial center is connected, and its Coxeter number is two, so $p=5$ satisfies the standing characteristic hypothesis. The cyclotomic tower is totally ramified, while $F/\mathbb Q_5$ is unramified quadratic; F04-009 therefore gives $d_{k_F}=2$.

On inertia, $\bar\varepsilon$ has order four, so $\chi=\bar\varepsilon^2$ is nontrivial and $H^0(G_F,k(\chi))=0$. By D02-002,
\[
H^2(G_F,k(\chi))\cong H^0(G_F,k(\chi^{-1}\bar\varepsilon))^\vee=H^0(G_F,k(\bar\varepsilon^{-1}))^\vee=0.
\]
Thus $\Psi_0=\Psi_2=\varnothing$. F01-021 gives $\dim_kH^1(G_F,k(\chi))=2$, so a nonzero extension class exists. By F03-008, choosing one is precisely the nonempty fine-open condition $(x_{\delta,1},x_{\delta,2})\ne(0,0)$, and it makes $K=U$ minimal in type $A_1$.

Here $\Phi_K=\Delta_K=\{\delta\}$ and $\operatorname{Ht}_K(\delta)=1$. Since $\delta\notin\Psi_0$, the terminal-coordinate row is $x_{\delta,d_F+d_{k_F}}=x_{\delta,4}=0$; since $\delta\notin\Psi_2$, the common row $s_\delta=0$ also occurs. There is no higher-root obstruction equation and no torus equation. COR11-001 places both linear rows in the fine and cone ideals, giving the displayed ring $B$ on both sides. Its Laurent-polynomial presentation proves smoothness, integrality, and dimension four; passage to the indicated nonempty open preserves dimension.

Finally, truncating a polynomial presentation through degree two fixes every generator of degree one. Starting from $A$, whose only displayed generator is $s_\delta$, truncation therefore returns $A$ and cannot produce $x_{\delta,4}$. Starting from the corrected fine presentation produces $B$ on both sides. The rings $A$ and $A/(x_{\delta,4})$ compare two different linear ideals, not a fine model with its cone.

# Statement


Let $F/\mathbb Q_p$ be finite, write $\operatorname{Gal}(F(\mu_{p^\infty})/F)=\Delta_F\times\Gamma$ with $\Gamma\cong\mathbb Z_p$, put $F_{\mathrm{cyc}}=F(\mu_{p^\infty})^{\Delta_F}$, and let $k_{F_{\mathrm{cyc}}}$ be its residue field. Let $\mathbf M\subsetneq\mathbf G$ be a Levi subgroup with maximal torus $\mathbf T$, let $w\in N_{\mathbf M}(\mathbf T)$ be elliptic of order $n$, and let $F_n/F$ be the unramified extension of degree $n$. Let $\mathbf K=\prod_{\boldsymbol\beta\in\boldsymbol\Phi_{\mathbf K}}U_{\boldsymbol\beta}\subset U^{\mathbf M}$ be $\mathbf T\langle w\rangle$-stable, and assume $p>h_{\mathbf G}$. Fix an ordering of $\boldsymbol\Phi_{\mathbf K}$ compatible with $\mathbf K$-height. For a relative root $\boldsymbol\alpha$, let $U_{\leq\boldsymbol\alpha}$ and $X_{\leq\boldsymbol\alpha}$ be the accumulated unipotent subquotient and recursive chart. For each $\boldsymbol\beta\leq\boldsymbol\alpha$, put $A_{\boldsymbol\beta}=\operatorname{Res}_{k_{F_{\mathrm{cyc}}}/\mathbb F_p}U_{\boldsymbol\beta}$ and assume that the object of $\Gamma$-stable and $\operatorname{Gal}(F_n/F)$-stable sections used at the $\boldsymbol\beta$ step is a genuine torsor under $A_{\boldsymbol\beta}$. Then
\[
X_{\leq\boldsymbol\alpha}\longrightarrow\mathcal X_{U_{\leq\boldsymbol\alpha}\rtimes\mathbf T\langle w\rangle}
\]
is smooth, surjective, and affine, and each geometric two-fiber is isomorphic to
\[
\operatorname{Res}_{k_{F_{\mathrm{cyc}}}/\mathbb F_p}U_{\leq\boldsymbol\alpha}\rtimes\mathbf T.
\]
If $\boldsymbol\alpha_{\max}$ is the last root in the ordering, then the fiber at $\boldsymbol\alpha_{\max}$ is $\operatorname{Res}_{k_{F_{\mathrm{cyc}}}/\mathbb F_p}\mathbf K\rtimes\mathbf T$. Under the additional equality $\mathbf K=U^{\mathbf M}$, the fiber at $\boldsymbol\alpha_{\max}$ is $\operatorname{Res}_{k_{F_{\mathrm{cyc}}}/\mathbb F_p}U^{\mathbf M}\rtimes\mathbf T$. For every integer $r$, the chart obtained after all relative roots of $\mathbf K$-height at most $r$ have been processed is independent up to canonical isomorphism of their ordering within each height. An individual equal-height intermediate stage need not be ordering-independent.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

The initial torus chart has geometric two-fiber $\mathbf T$. By hypothesis, the step for a relative root $\boldsymbol\beta$ is a torsor under the smooth affine Weil-restricted factor attached to $U_{\boldsymbol\beta}$. Base change and composition preserve smoothness, surjectivity, and affineness. Induction over the chosen ordering therefore identifies the fiber after the $\boldsymbol\alpha$ step with the iterated semidirect product of $\mathbf T$ and precisely the root-group factors accumulated in $U_{\leq\boldsymbol\alpha}$, namely
\[
\operatorname{Res}_{k_{F_{\mathrm{cyc}}}/\mathbb F_p}U_{\leq\boldsymbol\alpha}\rtimes\mathbf T.
\]
At the last root, the accumulated group is $\mathbf K$, giving the final formula and the stated specialization when $\mathbf K=U^{\mathbf M}$.

Root groups of the same $\mathbf K$-height commute in the relevant graded subquotient. Consequently their torsor base changes commute, so permuting all steps within one height gives canonically isomorphic charts after that entire height has been completed. Before all those factors have been accumulated, changing the order changes $U_{\leq\boldsymbol\alpha}$ and can change the fiber dimension, so no ordering-independence assertion is available for a fixed individual intermediate root.

# Statement


Let $\bB=U\rtimes\bT$ be a split Borel subgroup over $\bFp$, and let $\bar\rho:\Gal_F\to\bB(\bFp)$ be a Galois representation. The minimal $\bT$-stable normal subgroup attached to $\bar\rho$ is the least $\bT$-stable normal subgroup $K\subset U$ for which there is $b\in\bB(\bFp)$ such that $b\bar\rho b^{-1}$ factors through $(K\rtimes\bT)(\bFp)$.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

### Well-posedness

Let $\Gamma$ be the image of $\bar\rho$, and let $\mathcal A$ be the set of $\bT$-stable normal closed subgroups $K\subset U$ for which some $\bB$-conjugate of $\Gamma$ is contained in $K\rtimes\bT$. This set is nonempty because $U\in\mathcal A$. Since $U$ is affine and Noetherian, descending chains of closed subgroup schemes stabilize: they give ascending chains of defining ideals in $\mathcal O(U)$. Hence $\mathcal A$ has an inclusion-minimal member $K_0$.

For completeness, the intersection property applies exactly in this Galois setting. By the usual continuity convention for a mod-$p$ Galois representation, $\Gamma$ is finite. Given $K_1,K_2\in\mathcal A$ and corresponding conjugating elements, all involved points are defined over one finite subfield $k\subset\overline{\mathbb F}_p$. In $\bB(k)=U(k)\rtimes\bT(k)$, the first factor is a finite $p$-group and the second has order prime to $p$. The Schur-Zassenhaus argument of F03-001 therefore gives a conjugate of $\Gamma$ contained in $(K_1\cap K_2)\rtimes\bT$, so $K_1\cap K_2\in\mathcal A$.

For any $K\in\mathcal A$, apply this to $K_0$ and $K$. Their intersection belongs to $\mathcal A$ and is contained in $K_0$, so minimality forces $K_0\cap K=K_0$. Thus $K_0\subset K$ for every $K\in\mathcal A$. It is the unique least admissible subgroup, proving that the definition is meaningful.

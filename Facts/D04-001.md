# Statement


Let $A$ be a commutative ring and let $f_1,\ldots,f_k\in A$ be nonzerodivisors. Put $N=\bigoplus_{i=1}^k A/(f_i)$. On the category of $A$-algebras, the functor $S\mapsto\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(N,S)$ is represented by
\[
X=\operatorname{Spec} A[s_1,\ldots,s_k]/(f_1s_1,\ldots,f_ks_k)
 \cong X_1\times_{\operatorname{Spec}A}\cdots\times_{\operatorname{Spec}A}X_k,
\qquad X_i=\operatorname{Spec}A[s_i]/(f_is_i).
\]
If the ideals $(f_i)$ are pairwise comaximal, then $s_is_j=0$ in $A[s_1,\ldots,s_k]/(f_1s_1,\ldots,f_ks_k)$ for $i\ne j$, and $X$ is also the affine scheme-theoretic pushout obtained by gluing the $X_i$ along their common zero section $\operatorname{Spec}A$.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

For each $i$, the exact sequence $0\to A\xrightarrow{f_i}A\to A/(f_i)\to0$ is a free resolution. Tensoring with an $A$-algebra $S$ gives
\[
\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(A/(f_i),S)=\ker(f_i:S\to S)=\{s_i\in S:f_is_i=0\}.
\]
First Tor commutes with finite direct sums, so $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(N,S)$ is naturally the set of tuples $(s_1,\ldots,s_k)$ satisfying $f_is_i=0$ for every $i$. The universal property of a polynomial algebra gives the stated representing ring, and tensoring the individual coordinate rings over $A$ gives the asserted fiber product of schemes.

If $(f_i)+(f_j)=A$, choose $a,b\in A$ with $af_i+bf_j=1$. Then in the representing ring,
\[s_is_j=(af_i+bf_j)s_is_j=0.\]
Thus all mixed products vanish. The fiber product ring is consequently isomorphic to the iterated ring fiber product of the $A[s_i]/(f_is_i)$ over their zero-section quotients to $A$; contravariantly, this is the affine pushout gluing the $X_i$ along $\operatorname{Spec}A$.

## Post-proof remarks

No internal issues. For each nonzerodivisor f_i, the two-term free resolution computes Tor_1(A/(f_i),S) as the annihilator of f_i in S, and finite direct sums give exactly the tuple functor represented by A[s_1,...,s_k]/(f_i s_i). The tensor product of the one-axis coordinate rings gives the stated scheme fiber product. Under pairwise comaximality, a f_i+b f_j=1 implies s_i s_j=0; consequently the coordinate ring is A plus the zero-section ideals with all mixed products zero, which is precisely the iterated ring fiber product over A and hence the affine pushout of the axes. The pairwise-comaximal hypothesis is the exact condition missing from the repeated-support case in parent F01-020. The proof is self-contained, source-bounded, and has no dependencies.

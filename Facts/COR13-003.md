# Statement


Let $F/\mathbb Q_p$ be finite. Let $\mathbf G$ be a split connected reductive group over $\overline{\mathbb Z}_p$ with connected center, maximal torus $\mathbf T$, simple roots $\Delta$, and Coxeter number $h_{\mathbf G}$, and assume $p>h_{\mathbf G}$. Let $\mathbf M\subsetneq\mathbf G$ be the standard Levi defined by $\Delta_M\subsetneq\Delta$, let $U^{\mathbf M}$ be the unipotent radical of the corresponding standard parabolic, and choose a Tits representative $\dot w\in N_{\mathbf M}(\mathbf T)(\overline{\mathbb Z}_p)$ whose Weyl image $w$ is elliptic in $\mathbf M$. Write $n$ for the order of $\dot w$, let $v:G_F\to\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ be the unramified quotient normalized by arithmetic Frobenius, let $q_n$ be its reduction modulo $n$, and let $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}:G_F\to\mathbb Z_p^\times$ be the cyclotomic character. Choose a $w$-fixed cocharacter $\mu\in X_*(\mathbf T)$ with $\langle\gamma,\mu\rangle=0$ for $\gamma\in\Delta_M$ and $1$ for $\gamma\in\Delta\setminus\Delta_M$. Choose $t\in\mathbf T(\overline{\mathbb Z}_p)^w$ with $t^n=1$ such that, for every $\delta\in\Delta\setminus\Delta_M$, if $m_\delta=\#\langle w\rangle\delta$ and $\operatorname{Ad}(\dot w^{m_\delta})$ acts on the $\delta$-root line by $c_\delta$, then $c_\delta\,\delta(t)^{m_\delta}=1$. Define the integral normalizer representation $N_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g)=t^{v(g)}\,\mu(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g))\,\dot w^{q_n(g)}$. Let $\Phi^{\mathbf M+}$ be the positive roots in $U^{\mathbf M}$, put $\operatorname{Ht}^{\mathbf M}(\alpha)=\sum_{\delta\notin\Delta_M}n_\delta$ for $\alpha=\sum_{\delta\in\Delta}n_\delta\delta$, and define $\boldsymbol\Delta=\{\langle w\rangle\alpha:\alpha\in\Phi^{\mathbf M+},\ \operatorname{Ht}^{\mathbf M}(\alpha)=1\}$. Assume the orbit-normalizability map $\Delta\setminus\Delta_M\longrightarrow\boldsymbol\Delta$, $\delta\longmapsto\langle w\rangle\delta$, is surjective. For $\boldsymbol\alpha\in\boldsymbol\Delta$, define $U_{\boldsymbol\alpha}$ as the product of its root factors in the relative-height-one graded quotient of $U^{\mathbf M}$. Then, with the action induced by $N_{\mathrm{cyc}}$, $U_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\overline{\mathbb Z}_p)\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{G_{F_{m_{\boldsymbol\alpha}}}}^{G_F}\overline{\mathbb Z}_p(1)$ and $H^2(G_F,U_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\overline{\mathbb Z}_p))\cong\overline{\mathbb Z}_p$ for every $\boldsymbol\alpha\in\boldsymbol\Delta$, where $m_{\boldsymbol\alpha}=\#\boldsymbol\alpha$. Let $\underline\lambda_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ be the Hodge type of $N_{\mathrm{cyc}}$, let $X_{\mathbf T\langle\dot w\rangle}^{\underline\lambda_{\mathrm{cyc}},\mathrm{tame}}$ be a destackification of the corresponding torus-normalizer fixed-type stack, put $R=\mathcal O_{X_{\mathbf T\langle\dot w\rangle}^{\underline\lambda_{\mathrm{cyc}},\mathrm{tame}}}$, and let $\mathbf A_{F,R}$ be the integral period ring. The presheaf on $p$-adically complete $R$-algebras $S\longmapsto\prod_{\boldsymbol\alpha\in\boldsymbol\Delta}\operatorname{Tor}_1^R\bigl(H^2_{\mathrm{Herr}}(U_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\mathbf A_{F,R})),S\bigr)$ is represented over the fixed-type stack by a $p$-adic formal stack $\mathfrak W_{U^{\mathbf M}\rtimes\mathbf T\langle\dot w\rangle}$. Its rigid generic fiber is represented by prime-to-$p$ unramified descent from the destackification, and its special fiber is the mod-$p$ Weil-Deligne stack $\mathcal W_{U^{\mathbf M}\rtimes\mathbf T\langle\dot w\rangle}$.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

The orbit-normalizability hypothesis is used first and is not weakened. It makes every $\boldsymbol\alpha\in\boldsymbol\Delta$ equal to $\langle w\rangle\delta$ for an omitted simple root $\delta$. COR13-001 then identifies the stabilizer root line at the corrected cyclotomic point with $\overline{\mathbb Z}_p(1)$, including the finite unramified correction. Transitivity and Shapiro give

$$U_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\overline{\mathbb Z}_p)\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{G_{F_{m_{\boldsymbol\alpha}}}}^{G_F}\overline{\mathbb Z}_p(1),\qquad H^2(G_F,U_{\boldsymbol\alpha})\cong\overline{\mathbb Z}_p.$$

This proves the two lattice assertions for every orbit occurring in the Statement.

Work over a finite coefficient extension on an affine formal component of the torus-normalizer destackification. For $\boldsymbol\alpha=\langle w\rangle\delta$, restriction of the universal orbit family to $G_{F_{m_{\boldsymbol\alpha}}}$ is a coefficient line $L_{\boldsymbol\alpha}$ tensored with the cyclotomic character and a universal unramified character $u_{\boldsymbol\alpha}$. Its Cartier dual is the inverse unramified character. Family Shapiro and COR17-001 therefore give the canonical, base-change-compatible identity

$$H^2_{\mathrm{Herr}}(U_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\mathbf A_{F,R}))\cong L_{\boldsymbol\alpha}/f_{\boldsymbol\alpha}L_{\boldsymbol\alpha},\qquad f_{\boldsymbol\alpha}=u_{\boldsymbol\alpha}-1,$$

up to multiplication of $f_{\boldsymbol\alpha}$ by a unit. Here $f_{\boldsymbol\alpha}=0$ is exactly the pullback of the trivial-character Cartier divisor. This is a scheme-theoretic exponent-one calculation, not just a calculation of its closed-point support: it follows by dualizing top Herr cohomology to degree-zero cohomology of the Cartier dual, whose invariant equation is $u_{\boldsymbol\alpha}-1$, and COR17-001 checks the infinitesimal and rigid-family compatibility.

The equation is regular integrally and after reduction. Indeed, on the split torus chart $u_{\boldsymbol\alpha}$ is the orbit-norm Laurent character. Pairing its character with the omitted-direction cocharacter used in COR13-001 gives $m_{\boldsymbol\alpha}$; equivalently, on that one-parameter subtorus it is $z\mapsto z^{m_{\boldsymbol\alpha}}$. Thus it is nonconstant, so $u_{\boldsymbol\alpha}-1$ is a nonzerodivisor on every Laurent torus component. The same holds modulo $p$; moreover $m_{\boldsymbol\alpha}$ divides the common Weyl splitting degree, which is prime to $p$ by F04-015. Tensoring by the invertible line preserves regularity. Consequently every universal orbit module has the required length-one line-bundle resolution before and after reduction.

These identities are compatible with the finite descent data. Take the common unramified splitting extension $F_n/F$. Its cyclic Galois group permutes the split root factors, the lines $L_{\boldsymbol\alpha}$, and their orbit-norm characters. Because each $f_{\boldsymbol\alpha}$ is defined as the pullback of the identity divisor in the character torus, the displayed resolutions are carried to one another and satisfy the cocycle condition. The group order is a unit by F04-015. Applying COR16-003 to this finite equivariant family represents the product of completed $\operatorname{Tor}_1$ functors by the product of the formal line-kernels, descends it effectively through the finite prime-to-$p$ groupoid, and identifies its rigid generic fiber with the corresponding unramified quotient. The ordinary fpqc descent from the torus atlas to the fixed-type stack is effective because the construction and all equations are canonical under change of trivialization. This constructs the formal stack and proves the representability and rigid-descent clauses in the Statement.

Finally, reduction of each length-one resolution is exact and represents the full reduced $\operatorname{Tor}_1$ functor on arbitrary coefficient algebras, not only on geometric points. D04-002 identifies the split reduced factor with the simple cyclotomic Herr crossing. COR8-004 then identifies its prime-to-$p$ Galois descent with the twisted relative-root mod-$p$ Weil-Deligne stack. Taking the finite product over $\boldsymbol\Delta$ and descending the common torus atlas gives precisely $\mathcal W_{U^{\mathbf M}\rtimes\mathbf T\langle\dot w\rangle}$; the same quotient groupoids retain its stabilizers, and the functorial calculation includes nilpotent test algebras. COR16-003 shows that this product quotient is the special fiber of the integral formal stack. This proves the special-fiber clause.

The qualifier remains necessary. In type $G_2$, with Levi root $a$ and $w=s_a$, the relative-height-one roots form the two orbits $\{b,3a+b\}$ and $\{a+b,2a+b\}$, while only the first contains the omitted simple root $b$. Without the stated surjectivity hypothesis, COR13-001 and hence the universal Cartier calculation would not cover every factor in $\boldsymbol\Delta$.

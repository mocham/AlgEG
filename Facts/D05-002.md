# Statement


Let $G$ be a connected reductive group over a field $k$, let $M$ be a connected reductive $k$-group embedded in $G$, and suppose $F=\operatorname{Int}(g)$ is an automorphism of $M$ for some $g\in G(k)$. After base change to an algebraic closure, there exist an $F$-stable Borel subgroup $B_M\subset M$ and a scheme morphism $\mu:\mathbb G_m\to M$ such that the dynamic functor $P_G(\mu)$ is represented by a parabolic subgroup of $G$,
\[P_G(\mu)\cap M=P_M(\mu)=B_M,\]
and $g\in P_G(\mu)$.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

Steinberg gives an $F$-stable Borel $B_M$. Choose a regular cocharacter $\lambda$ with $P_M(\lambda)=B_M$. Lin22 Lemma F-finiteness supplies $d>0$ and $u$ in the unipotent radical of $B_M$ such that $F^d(\lambda)=u\lambda u^{-1}$. Set the fake cocharacter $\mu=F^{d-1}(\lambda)\cdots F(\lambda)\lambda$. Every factor defines the same Borel, so the generalized dynamic lemma makes $P_G(\mu)$ an ambient parabolic and gives $P_M(\mu)=B_M$, hence the displayed intersection. Moreover,
\[F(\mu)=(u\lambda u^{-1})\mu\lambda^{-1}.\]
Using $F=\operatorname{Int}(g)$, one rewrites $\mu g\mu^{-1}g^{-1}=\mu F(\mu)^{-1}$ as the product of the commutator of $\mu$ with $\lambda$ and the conjugate $\lambda u\lambda^{-1}u^{-1}$. The generalized dynamic lemma gives a limit for the first factor as $t\to0$, and $u$ belonging to the unipotent radical gives a limit for the second. Thus $\lim_{t\to0}\mu(t)g\mu(t)^{-1}$ exists, which is exactly $g\in P_G(\mu)$.

## Post-proof remarks

Checked the construction line by line against Lin22 lines 455-644, 647-684, and 686-760. The order of the finiteness relation can be reversed by replacing the unipotent element by its inverse. The product $\mu$ is intentionally only a scheme morphism, while the generalized dynamic lemma proves representability of its ambient parabolic. The limit computation establishing $g\in P_G(\mu)$ is valid and does not use the later irreducibility hypothesis, so this is strictly weaker than F09-020.

# Statement


Let $F/\mathbb Q_p$ be finite, let $\mathbf G$ be a connected reductive group with connected center, assume $p>h_{\mathbf G}$, and fix a Borel subgroup $\mathbf B=\mathbf T U$. Let $\mathcal X_{\mathbf G}^{\mathrm{EG}}$ be the Emerton-Gee stack. For a root configuration $\Psi$, let $\dot{\mathcal X}_{\mathbf B}\langle\Psi\rangle$ be a finite-type Borel-valued stratum such that, at every geometric point represented by $\bar\rho:G_F\to\mathbf B(\overline{\mathbb F}_p)$, the simple-root cocycle class $[c_\delta]\in H^1(G_F,U_{\delta^\vee}(\overline{\mathbb F}_p))$ is nonzero for every simple root $\delta$. If $Y$ is the scheme-theoretic image of the natural morphism $\dot{\mathcal X}_{\mathbf B}\langle\Psi\rangle\to\mathcal X_{\mathbf G}^{\mathrm{EG}}$, then $\dim Y\geq\dim\dot{\mathcal X}_{\mathbf B}\langle\Psi\rangle$.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

Let $x$ be a geometric point of $Y$ represented by a $\mathbf B$-valued representation $\bar\rho$. We first show that $Z_{\mathbf G}(\bar\rho)\subset\mathbf B$. Let $z\in Z_{\mathbf G}(\bar\rho)$ and write its Bruhat decomposition as $z=b\dot wv$, with $b\in\mathbf B$ and $v\in U_w$. For every $\gamma\in G_F$, centrality gives
\[
 \dot w^{-1}(b^{-1}\bar\rho(\gamma)b)\dot w
 =v\bar\rho(\gamma)v^{-1}\in\mathbf B.
\]
Suppose $w\ne1$. By the Weyl descent used in F05-011, there is a simple root $\delta$ such that $w^{-1}(\delta)$ is negative. Conjugation by $b$ rescales the simple-root extension class and changes its cocycle representative by a coboundary, so the resulting $\delta$-class is still nonzero. On the other hand, the displayed containment in $\mathbf B$ forces the $\delta$-coordinate of $b^{-1}\bar\rho b$ to vanish for every $\gamma$: after conjugation by $\dot w^{-1}$ it is the unique contribution in the negative root space $\mathfrak g_{w^{-1}(\delta)}$, since $\delta$ is simple. Its cohomology class would therefore be zero, a contradiction. Hence $w=1$ and $z\in\mathbf B$. Thus $Z_{\mathbf G}(\bar\rho)=Z_{\mathbf B}(\bar\rho)$.

The geometric fiber of the Borel-to-$\mathbf G$ morphism over $x$, restricted to the stated stratum, embeds in the quotient $Z_{\mathbf G}(\bar\rho)/Z_{\mathbf B}(\bar\rho)$, which is a point. Every geometric fiber over the scheme-theoretic image consequently has dimension at most zero. The standard fiber-dimension inequality for finite-type algebraic stacks gives
\[
 \dim\dot{\mathcal X}_{\mathbf B}\langle\Psi\rangle\leq\dim Y,
\]
as required.

## Faithful GrobV14 proof

```tex
\begin{proof}
It suffices to show that every geometric fiber over $Y$ has dimension
zero.  We use the following sublemma.

\begin{sublem}
\label{fact:F05-011}
Let $g = tu \in \bB$
where $t\in \bT$ and $u\in U$,
such that $\log_{\le h_{\bG}} u = \sum_{\alpha \in \Phi^+} u_{\alpha}$. If $u_{\delta} \neq 0$ for all simple roots $\delta \in \Delta$, then $Z_{\bG}(u) \subset \bB$.
\end{sublem}

\begin{proof}
Let $z=b\dot wv\in Z_{\bG}(u)$ be its Bruhat decomposition, with
$b\in\bB$ and $v\in U_w$.  Then
\[
\dot w^{-1}(b^{-1}ub)\dot w=vuv^{-1}.
\]
Conjugation by $b$ preserves $U$ and acts by a nonzero scalar on each
simple-root quotient of $U/[U,U]$.  Thus every simple-root component of
$\log_{\le h_{\bG}}(b^{-1}ub)$ is nonzero, whereas the right hand side
has logarithm in $\operatorname{Lie}U$.  If $w\ne1$, there is a simple
root $\delta$ such that $w^{-1}(\delta)$ is negative.  Its nonzero
component is then carried to $\mathfrak g_{w^{-1}(\delta)}$, a
contradiction.  Hence $w=1$ and $z\in\bB$.
\end{proof}

\EDITXIV{Now let $x$ be a geometric point of $Y$, represented by
$\bar\rho:\Gal_F\to\bB(\bFp)$.  If $z\in Z_{\bG}(\bar\rho)$, the same
Bruhat calculation applies with the nonzero simple-root cohomology
classes $[c_\delta]$ in place of the components $u_\delta$.  If $w\ne1$,
conjugation by $b$ preserves the nonzero class $[c_\delta]$, while the
negative root space for $w^{-1}(\delta)$ forces that class to be zero.
Thus $Z_{\bG}(\bar\rho)=Z_{\bB}(\bar\rho)$.  The fiber over $x$ embeds in
\[
Z_{\bG}(\bar\rho)/Z_{\bB}(\bar\rho)=\{1\}.
\]
The fiber-dimension inequality gives
$\dim\dot\cX_{\bB}\langle\Psi\rangle\leq\dim Y$.}
\end{proof}
```

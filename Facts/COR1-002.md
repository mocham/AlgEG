# Statement


Let $F_n/F$ be the unramified extension of degree $n$, put $H=G_{F_n}\triangleleft G_F$, and choose $\sigma\in G_F$ whose image generates $G_F/H$. Let $\mathbf G$ be a connected reductive group over $\overline{\mathbb Z}_p$ with maximal torus $\mathbf T$, choose $\dot w\in N_{\mathbf G}(\mathbf T)(\overline{\mathbb Z}_p)$ whose Weyl image has order $n$, and let $\rho:H\to\mathbf T(\overline{\mathbb Z}_p)$ be a continuous character. Define
\[
\psi(g)=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\dot w^i\rho(\sigma^{-i}g\sigma^i)\dot w^{-i}\qquad(g\in H).
\]
Assume the descent compatibility $\psi(\sigma^n)=\dot w^n$. Every $h\in G_F$ has a unique expression $h=g\sigma^k$ with $g\in H$ and $0\le k<n$, and the rule
\[
N(h)=N(g\sigma^k):=\psi(g)\dot w^k
\]
defines a continuous homomorphism $N:G_F\to\mathbf T(\overline{\mathbb Z}_p)\langle\dot w\rangle$. In particular, for a cocharacter $\lambda\in X_*(\mathbf T)$ and the Lubin-Tate character $\chi_{\mathrm{LT}}:H\to\mathcal O_{F_n}^\times$, one may take $\rho=\chi_{\mathrm{LT}}^\lambda$. The normalized conditions $\dot w^n=1$ and $\chi_{\mathrm{LT}}(\sigma^n)=1$ are sufficient for the descent compatibility.

# Proof or Examples


## Expanded self-contained proof

The product defining $\psi$ is a homomorphism because all its factors take values in the abelian group $\mathbf T$. Put $\theta=\operatorname{Ad}(\dot w)|_{\mathbf T}$. Since the Weyl image of $\dot w$ has order $n$, the element $\dot w^n$ lies in $\mathbf T$, and hence $\theta^n=1$. Also $\sigma^n\in H$. For $g\in H$, reindexing the product and using commutativity of $\rho(H)$ gives
\[
\psi(\sigma g\sigma^{-1})=\theta(\psi(g)).
\]
Indeed, the term that crosses the end of the product is unchanged because $\rho(\sigma^{-n}g\sigma^n)=\rho(\sigma^n)^{-1}\rho(g)\rho(\sigma^n)=\rho(g)$.

The quotient $G_F/H$ is cyclic of order $n$, so the representatives $g\sigma^k$ with $0\le k<n$ are unique. If $k+\ell<n$, the displayed equivariance immediately gives
\[
N((g\sigma^k)(h\sigma^\ell))=\psi(g)\dot w^k\psi(h)\dot w^\ell.
\]
If $k+\ell\ge n$, reducing the exponent to the chosen range introduces the factor $\sigma^n$ on the $H$ side. Its image is $\psi(\sigma^n)$, while the unreduced product introduces $\dot w^n$; these agree by hypothesis. Thus the same equality holds in every case, proving multiplicativity. Continuity follows from continuity on $H$ and the finite coset decomposition.

For $\rho=\chi_{\mathrm{LT}}^\lambda$, the normalized conditions imply compatibility because each conjugate of $\sigma^n$ is itself and $\chi_{\mathrm{LT}}^\lambda(\sigma^n)=1$, so $\psi(\sigma^n)=1=\dot w^n$. The unrestricted integer-exponent calculation in C01-004 violates both the unique-representative convention and this compatibility, and therefore illustrates why they must be stated.
